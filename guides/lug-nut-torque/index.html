Lug nut torque: how a mechanic tightens wheels and when to recheck

Torque wrench, star pattern, clean dry threads and a recheck within 100 miles or 2 to 3 days: what USTMA, Toyota and NHTSA filed guidance say about lug nuts.

Last updated: November 12, 2026

By Smart Tire Picks Editorial Team

This page is for general information only and is not professional, safety, or legal advice. Always confirm tire size, load index, speed rating, and other specs against your vehicle’s door placard and owner’s manual, and have tires installed by a licensed professional. See our Disclaimer.

The short answer

Use the torque the vehicle manufacturer specifies, start every nut by hand, tighten in a star pattern, finish with a calibrated torque wrench (or a torque stick that matches the specification), keep the threads clean and dry, and check the torque again within 100 miles or 2 to 3 days. Every one of those steps comes from the sources listed below. This page does not give a torque figure, because the correct number depends on the vehicle and the fastener and is in the owner's manual or repair manual.

Where to find the specification

USTMA's Care and Service guide tells installers to consult the vehicle owner's manual for recommendations on tire and wheel assembly installation and to identify the lug torque specification. The wheel installation instructions that TAP Worldwide filed with NHTSA add an order of precedence: use the original equipment torque specification, ask the vehicle manufacturer if the manual does not list one, and only then check with the aftermarket wheel maker. If you fit aftermarket wheels or lug nuts, the same instructions say to check that the fasteners have the right seat and size for the wheel.

Step by step at the bay

  1. Inspect the studs and seats. The TAP Worldwide instructions say to clean and inspect stud threads and mounting surfaces, that threads must not be lubricated, and that corroded, stripped or damaged fasteners should be replaced.
  2. Seat the wheel and start by hand. Make sure the wheel is centered and supported so it does not hang on the studs during initial tightening, and check that no nut bottoms out on the stud. A nut that bottoms out does not clamp the wheel.
  3. Snug in a star pattern. Fasteners must be tightened in a star or crisscross pattern for uniform pressure. Repeat the pattern until the specified torque is reached.
  4. Finish with a torque wrench. USTMA says to always use a torque wrench for final tightening. Toyota allows a torque wrench or a torque stick, and Toyota's 2013 tire rotation tech tip says the final torque should come from a torque wrench and not an impact tool.
  5. Write down the specification and tell the customer to recheck. The instructions say to perform a torque check within the first 100 miles or 2 to 3 days and any time a fastener has been removed.

Too tight and too loose both cause problems

USTMA says improper torque, either too loose or too tight, may cause loss of lug nuts, breaking of studs, damage to the tire and wheel assembly, or detachment of the assembly from the axle. Toyota's technical bulletin on brake vibration says over torqued lug nuts can give the rotor a runout condition, and that even one nut over or under torqued can cause a vibration condition over time. AAA's Extra Mile column describes a customer who could not loosen nuts at the roadside after a shop overtightened them, and notes that overtightening can warp brake rotors on some vehicles. A vibration complaint after a tire service is a good reason to recheck the torque and the tightening pattern before blaming balance. For balance and alignment, see balancing vs alignment.

Impact tools and torque sticks

An impact gun is fine for running nuts down, but the sources are consistent that it should not set the final torque. Toyota says to apply torque with a torque wrench or torque stick and to keep the tools calibrated on a regular schedule. AAA recommends torque sticks in shops that use impact wrenches because they limit the force. USTMA says never to use air tools to install custom wheels. A torque stick only limits torque at its rated value, so pick one that matches the vehicle specification and still verify the final nuts with a wrench.

Frequently asked questions

What torque should I use on lug nuts?

The one the vehicle manufacturer gives. USTMA says to consult the owner's manual, identify the lug torque specification and tighten lug nuts and bolts to the exact figure with a torque wrench. Toyota's technical bulletin points technicians to the model repair manual for the value, and the wheel instructions filed with NHTSA by TAP Worldwide say to use the original equipment torque specification and to ask the vehicle maker if the manual does not list one. We do not publish a generic number because it varies by vehicle and fastener.

Why use a star pattern instead of going around the wheel?

A star or crisscross pattern pulls the wheel flat against the hub. TAP Worldwide's instructions say fasteners must be tightened in a star or crisscross pattern for uniform pressure and alignment, and repeated until the specified torque is reached. Toyota's brake vibration bulletin and its 2013 tire rotation tech tip, both filed with NHTSA, also specify a star pattern.

Can I finish tightening with an impact gun?

Not as the final step on the torque figure. Toyota's 2013 tire rotation tech tip, filed with NHTSA, says the final lug nut torque should be reached with a torque wrench and not with an impact tool. Toyota says to use a torque wrench or a torque stick, and AAA says shops that use impact wrenches should use torque sticks to prevent overtightening. USTMA says never to use air tools to install custom wheels.

Should lug nut threads be oiled or greased?

No. The wheel installation instructions filed with NHTSA by TAP Worldwide say threads must not be lubricated and must be free of corrosion, rust, burrs and damage, and that damaged fasteners should be replaced. Torque figures assume the friction of dry, clean fasteners.

When should lug nuts be rechecked?

Those same instructions say to perform a torque check within the first 100 miles or 2 to 3 days, and any time a fastener is removed for any reason. USTMA says lug nuts should be properly torqued anytime a tire and wheel assembly is reinstalled on the vehicle.

What happens if lug nuts are too tight?

Toyota says over torqued lug nuts can give the brake rotor a runout condition that leads to brake vibration, and that even one lug nut over or under torqued can cause it over time. AAA adds that overtightened nuts can be impossible to remove with the short wrench that comes with the car and can warp brake rotors on some vehicles. USTMA lists breaking studs and damage to the assembly among the risks of incorrect torque.

What happens if lug nuts are too loose?

USTMA says torque that is too loose or too tight may cause loss of lug nuts, breaking of studs, damage to the tire and wheel assembly, or detachment of the assembly from the axle. This is why the recheck and a calibrated tool matter.

How often should the torque wrench be calibrated?

Toyota's bulletin says torque wrenches and torque sticks should be calibrated on a regular schedule, and TAP Worldwide's instructions say a calibrated torque device must be used. Follow the tool maker's calibration interval and your shop procedure.
