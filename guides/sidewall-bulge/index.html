Tire sidewall bulge: is it safe to drive?

Short answer: no. A bulge or bubble that sticks out of the sidewall usually means the inner structure is damaged, often from hitting a pothole or curb, and the tire can fail suddenly. It cannot be repaired, so the tire needs replacing. A narrow inward dent that runs from rim to tread is different: it is usually a harmless sidewall indentation.

Why it matters: the U.S. Tire Manufacturers Association (USTMA) lists bulges as a sign of possible internal separation and warns that driving on damaged tires is dangerous because a damaged tire can suddenly fail. Continental says bulges, bubbles and sidewall deformities indicate internal structural failure and need immediate replacement, and Pirelli warns a bulging tire could burst and cause loss of control.

What causes a sidewall bulge?

  • Impact. Hankook and Uniroyal both say a sidewall bubble is often the result of hitting a pothole, curb or other object. Continental adds that impact damage often hurts the internal belts or sidewall even when nothing is visible outside.
  • Telltale signs of a hit. Look for a scuff or scrape on the sidewall near the bulge, or a dent on the wheel rim near it. A technician can also check the inner liner for a cut or bruise once the tire is off the wheel (Hankook, Uniroyal).
  • Low pressure makes hits worse. An underinflated sidewall flexes more and is easier to pinch against the rim. Keep pressure at your door placard value and check it monthly with our cold tire pressure guide.
  • Load and speed. Pirelli notes that high speed, overloading, rough roads and hard braking all add stress to a weakened tire and raise the chance of a blowout.

What to do if you find a bulge

  1. Stop driving on that tire as soon as it is safe. Pirelli’s advice is to change to the spare, or call a tow truck if you do not have one.
  2. Fit the spare and respect its limits. Most temporary spares are speed and distance limited; see our spare tire (donut) limits guide. A sealant kit will not fix a bulge.
  3. Get the tire and wheel inspected. Ask the shop to check the rim for bends or dents too. USTMA’s used tire bulletin flags tires mounted on a bent, dented or cracked rim as unsuitable for service.
  4. Replace the tire. If you replace just one, read can you mix tires / replace 2 or 4, which matters most on all wheel drive vehicles.
  5. Check the others. The same pothole often hits two tires on one side. Look over every sidewall, check tread with our tread depth guide, and check age with the DOT date code decoder.

Can a sidewall bulge be repaired?

No. Continental says sidewall damage is never repairable because it is the structural area of the tire, and that bulges, bubbles and sidewall deformities require immediate replacement. Pirelli says a bulge cannot be safely fixed because the tire’s structure is no longer stable. Industry repair rules only allow small punctures in the tread area; our tire plug vs patch guide explains those limits.

Is a sidewall bulge covered by warranty?

Usually not when it comes from an impact. The Bridgestone and Firestone limited warranty manual lists road hazards, including impact breaks, bruises and bulges, as not covered. Uniroyal says a tire with evidence of impact damage is not covered by its warranty, though it still asks owners to bring the tire to a participating dealer for inspection. Hankook says many of its passenger tires carry a limited road hazard warranty, so check your own tire’s paperwork, and any road hazard plan you bought from the shop, before paying for a replacement.

A manufacturing defect is a different case from impact damage, and only a dealer inspection can tell them apart. Take the tire in rather than throwing it away.

How to lower the risk

  • Keep tires at the placard pressure, checked cold, at least monthly. Our TPMS warning light guide explains what the dash light does and does not catch.
  • Slow down for potholes you cannot avoid, and avoid braking hard while the wheel is in the hole.
  • Approach curbs slowly and square on; do not scrape the sidewall when parking.
  • Do not overload the vehicle beyond the placard limit or the tire’s load index.
  • Look at every sidewall when you check pressure, and see our when to replace tires guide for the other warning signs.

FAQ

Is it safe to drive with a bulge in the tire sidewall?

No. USTMA lists bulges as a sign of possible internal separation and warns a damaged tire can suddenly fail. Continental says bulges and bubbles need immediate replacement, and Pirelli advises changing to the spare or calling a tow.

Can a tire sidewall bulge be repaired?

No. Continental says sidewall damage is never repairable, and Pirelli says a bulge cannot be safely fixed because the tire structure is no longer stable. Only small punctures in the tread area can be considered for repair.

What is the difference between a sidewall bulge and a sidewall dent?

A bulge sticks out and usually means damaged cords inside the tire. A narrow inward dent running from rim toward tread is a sidewall indentation, which USTMA calls a cosmetic characteristic of radial construction that does not affect performance.

What causes a bubble on the side of a tire?

Most often an impact with a pothole, curb or other object, according to Hankook and Uniroyal. Signs include a scuff near the bubble, a dent on the wheel rim, or a cut or bruise on the inner liner once the tire is removed.

Does tire warranty cover a sidewall bulge?

Usually not if it came from an impact. The Bridgestone and Firestone limited warranty excludes road hazards including bulges, and Uniroyal excludes tires with impact damage. Some tires or shop plans include road hazard coverage, so have a dealer inspect it.

How long can I drive on a tire with a sidewall bubble?

There is no safe distance to plan on. Pirelli says it is only a matter of time before a bulging tire blows, so fit the spare as soon as it is safe and replace the tire.
