XL vs SL tires: what Extra Load, Standard Load and RF mean

Short answer: SL (Standard Load) is the normal version of a tire size. XL (Extra Load), also marked REINF or RF on Euro metric tires, can carry more weight, but only at a higher inflation pressure. Whichever you fit, set cold pressure from your vehicle placard, never below it, and never above the maximum on the sidewall.

Last updated: October 30, 2026

By Smart Tire Picks Editorial Team

What the markings mean

  • SL or no marking: Standard Load. Toyo notes that when a sidewall has no Reinforced or Extra Load marking, the tire is a standard load tire.
  • XL or EXTRA LOAD: a tire with a higher load rating than is usual for the same size, so it can work at higher pressures (Michelin).
  • REINF, REINFORCED or RF: the Euro metric term. Bridgestone says Reinforced means the same as Extra Load for Euro metric tires.
  • RF inside the size, such as 245/45RF19: not a load marking. The European Commission explains that an F before the rim diameter, plus a pictogram, marks a self supporting run flat tire.
  • C after the size: a van or light commercial tire, a separate category from XL (Michelin).

The load index number in the service description, such as the 94 in 205/55R16 94V, gives the maximum load. See our load index and speed rating guide for the chart.

How XL changes load and pressure

Bridgestone explains that an XL tire offers a higher maximum load than the SL version of the same size, but needs higher inflation pressure to reach it. The USTMA load table shows what that looks like in pounds per tire:

Tire32 psi35 psi38 psi41 psi44 psi
205/55R16 91 (SL)12241312135613561356
205/55R16 94 (XL)11791268135614331477

Source: USTMA, load capacity in lb. At 35 psi the XL tire actually carries a little less than the SL tire, even though its load index is higher. It only pulls ahead from 38 psi up. Bridgestone warns about exactly this: a higher load index on a Euro metric XL tire is not always proof it can carry the load at your pressure.

Minimum pressure for maximum load

Tire typeStandard LoadExtra Load
P metric (size starts with P)35 psi (240 kPa)41 psi (280 kPa)
Euro metric (no P)36 psi (250 kPa)42 psi (290 kPa)

Source: Bridgestone. Bridgestone also notes that some XL tires are branded with a higher maximum, such as 50 or 51 psi, but carry no more load above 41 psi (P metric) or 42 psi (Euro metric). The two size systems use different load tables, so compare like with like.

Why the vehicle placard still rules

USTMA is clear that the pressure molded on the sidewall is the maximum the tire can hold, regardless of vehicle or load, and is not the pressure to run. Typical sidewall maximums are 35, 36, 44 or 51 psi for Standard Load tires and 41, 42 or 50 psi for Extra Load tires. Your starting point is the cold pressure on the door placard or in the owner's manual, and you should never set pressure below it. Read more in our cold tire pressure guide.

When you need XL tires

  • The vehicle maker specifies it. If the placard or manual lists an XL size or a load index only XL tires reach, stay with XL.
  • Plus sizing. Continental says a replacement must carry at least the original load at the placard pressure. A lower profile size may need XL construction, and sometimes a higher pressure, to get there. See plus sizing.
  • Swapping SL for XL. Continental gives these pressure changes when an XL tire replaces the original SL tire:
XL load index vs original SLPressure change
Same as the original SL tireAdd 0.4 bar (6 psi)
Original plus 1Add 0.3 bar (4 psi)
Original plus 2Add 0.2 bar (3 psi)
Original plus 3Add 0.1 bar (2 psi)
Original plus 4 or moreSame pressure as the original

Never exceed the sidewall maximum, and keep any front to rear pressure difference the vehicle maker specifies. If the new pressure differs from the placard, note it so anyone who checks the tires later uses the right figure. For sizes, start with how to choose the right tire size.

Frequently asked questions

What does XL mean on a tire?

XL stands for Extra Load. An XL tire is built to carry more weight than the standard load version of the same size, but it only reaches that extra capacity at a higher inflation pressure. Euro metric tires may say Reinforced, REINF or RF instead; these mean the same thing as Extra Load.

What does SL mean, and what if there is no marking?

SL stands for Standard Load. Many standard load tires carry no load marking at all, so a passenger tire without XL, Extra Load or Reinforced on the sidewall is a standard load tire.

Is RF on a tire reinforced or run flat?

It depends where it appears. As a separate marking next to the size, RF means reinforced, the same as XL. When the letters RF sit inside the size itself before the rim diameter, as in 245/45RF19, they mark a self supporting run flat tire.

Should I inflate XL tires to the pressure on the sidewall?

No. The pressure molded on the sidewall is the maximum the tire can hold, not a setting for your vehicle. Start from the cold pressure on the vehicle placard, never go below it, and never exceed the sidewall maximum.

Can I put XL tires on a car that came with SL tires?

Usually yes, as long as the replacement carries at least the original load capacity at the pressure you run. Continental advises a higher pressure when an XL tire replaces an SL tire with the same or a slightly higher load index; see the table above, and ask the installer to confirm.

Do XL tires always carry more weight?

Not at every pressure. In the USTMA load tables, a 205/55R16 94 XL carries slightly less than a 205/55R16 91 SL at 35 psi and only pulls ahead from 38 psi up. That is why a higher load index alone does not prove an XL tire is a suitable swap.

When do I need XL tires?

When the vehicle maker specifies XL or a load index that only an XL tire offers in your size, and often when plus sizing to a lower profile tire that would otherwise carry less load than the original. Check the placard and owner's manual first.
