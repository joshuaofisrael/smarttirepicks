All weather vs all season tires: which do you need?

Short answer: both stay on your car all year, but an all weather tire carries the three peak mountain snowflake (3PMSF) symbol, meaning it passed a standardized snow traction test, while most all season tires carry only an M+S marking that involves no snow test at all. If you see real snow most winters and want one set of tires, all weather is usually the better fit. If winters are mild, a good all season tire is enough. If you face frequent ice or long deep cold, dedicated winter tires still win.

The real difference is on the sidewall

The names sound alike and tire ads blur them, so start with the markings. M+S (mud and snow) comes from a U.S. Tire Manufacturers Association (USTMA) definition based on tread design features such as groove and void area. It does not require the tire to pass any snow traction test, which is why Canada’s CAA says tires marked only M+S are not winter tires.

The 3PMSF symbol, a three peak mountain with a snowflake inside, is different. Under USTMA’s severe snow definition, a tire may carry it only after reaching a minimum snow traction index against a standard reference tire in a packed snow test. Dedicated winter tires carry it, and so do all weather tires. That one symbol is what separates an all weather tire from an ordinary all season tire.

Consumer Reports describes all weather tires as a subset of all season tires: they still sit in the all season, SUV all season and similar categories, but with tread and rubber tuned for cold and snow. Look for the symbol on the exact size you buy, since markings can vary across sizes in one model line.

Trade offs to know before you switch

  • Snow grip vs ice grip. The 3PMSF test is run on packed snow. It says nothing about glare ice, so an all weather tire is not a substitute for a studless winter tire where ice is a weekly event. See our winter tires guide for when dedicated winter tires make sense.
  • Not every all weather tire is equal. Consumer Reports notes that some all weather models performed well in its tests while others are best avoided, so compare independent results for the specific model.
  • Wear and warranty. Snow focused tread and compounds can trade away some tread life. Compare the UTQG treadwear grade and the mileage warranty, and remember that winter traction drops as tread wears; BC requires at least 3.5 mm of tread for a legal winter tire. Our tread depth guide shows how to measure it.
  • Price. Consumer Reports says some all weather tires cost more than the all season tires they replace, but most buyers come out ahead by skipping a second set and the seasonal changeovers.
  • Fit four, keep them matched. Whatever you choose, use the same type on all four wheels. Mixing all season and all weather tires front to rear can upset handling in snow. See mixing tires.
  • Pressure drops with temperature. Check pressure monthly when tires are cold, and more often as fall turns to winter. Our cold tire pressure guide explains how.

Winter tire rules that treat these tires differently

Most U.S. states do not require winter tires, but chain or traction rules can apply on mountain routes, and Canada is stricter:

  • Quebec. From December 1 to March 15 inclusive, vehicles registered in Quebec must run tires marked with the mountain and snowflake pictogram, or studded tires. Fines run from $200 to $300 plus costs. An M+S only all season tire does not comply; an all weather tire with 3PMSF does.
  • British Columbia. On signed highways during the winter tire period, a legal winter tire needs at least 3.5 mm of tread and either the M+S mark or the 3PMSF symbol. The province notes that 3PMSF tires, which include all weather tires, offer better traction on snow and ice and recommends them for cold weather driving.
  • Transport Canada advises that tires with the pictogram meet specific snow traction performance requirements and recommends four matching winter tires.

If you drive into Canada or over mountain passes in winter, check the rules for every area on your route before you go.

One set of all weather tires or two sets?

Running all season tires in summer and winter tires in winter means two sets of tires, often a second set of wheels, a place to store the off season set, and two changeovers a year. You do get the best tire for each season, and each set wears only part of the year. A single set of all weather tires removes the swaps and storage but asks one tire to do every job reasonably well. If you go the two set route, our seasonal tire storage guide covers how to store the off season set so it ages well.

Shopping checklist

  1. Confirm size, load index and speed rating on your door placard; see our load index and speed rating guide.
  2. Look for the 3PMSF symbol on the exact size if you want an all weather tire, not just M+S.
  3. Compare independent test results for the specific model, plus UTQG treadwear and the mileage warranty.
  4. Check the DOT date code so you are not sold old stock.
  5. Buy four and have them installed by a licensed professional.

FAQ

What is the difference between all weather and all season tires?

All weather tires carry the three peak mountain snowflake (3PMSF) symbol, meaning they passed a standardized packed snow traction test against a reference tire. Most all season tires carry only the M+S marking, which is based on tread geometry, not a snow test. Both are meant to stay on the car all year.

Are all weather tires good in snow?

Better than typical all season tires, because they meet the same 3PMSF snow traction requirement as dedicated winter tires. Consumer Reports says they typically do not match dedicated winter tires but come close enough for most needs. The 3PMSF test is on packed snow, so it does not rate ice grip.

Do all weather tires wear out faster than all season tires?

Often somewhat, because the softer, cold friendly compound and snow biting tread trade some wear for winter grip, but it varies a lot by model. Compare the UTQG treadwear grade and the mileage warranty of the exact tires you are considering.

Can I use all weather tires in summer?

Yes. All weather tires are designed to stay on year round. They are usually not as sharp in hot, dry handling as a performance all season or summer tire, so drivers of sporty cars in warm climates may prefer an all season or summer tire.

Do all weather tires count as winter tires in Quebec and British Columbia?

In Quebec, tires must carry the 3PMSF pictogram (or be studded) from December 1 to March 15, so all weather tires with the symbol comply and M+S only all season tires do not. British Columbia accepts M+S or 3PMSF tires with at least 3.5 mm of tread on signed highways, and recommends 3PMSF tires for cold weather.

Are all weather tires worth it?

They make the most sense if you see regular snow but not long stretches of deep cold and ice, and you do not want to buy, store and swap a second set. In mild climates a good all season tire is usually enough; in harsh winters dedicated winter tires are still the safer choice.
