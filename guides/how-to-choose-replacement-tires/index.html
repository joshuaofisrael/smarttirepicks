How to choose replacement tires

Start from the door placard, not the old tire. Buy the same size (or a maker approved option) with a load index that is not lower and a speed rating that is equal or higher, keep the same construction, then choose the tire type for your weather. Replace all four if you can; if you buy two, they go on the rear axle.

Last updated: November 17, 2026

By Smart Tire Picks Editorial Team

This page is for general information only and is not professional, safety, or legal advice. Always confirm tire size, load index, speed rating, and other specs against your vehicle’s door placard and owner’s manual, and have tires installed by a licensed professional. The short answer

Start from the door placard, not the old tire. Buy the same size (or a maker approved option) with a load index that is not lower and a speed rating that is equal or higher, keep the same construction, then choose the tire type for your weather. Replace all four if you can; if you buy two, they go on the rear axle. Every rule below comes from NHTSA, USTMA or Michelin pages we opened for this guide; your vehicle maker's instructions win if they differ.

Step 1: copy the placard, not the old tire

NHTSA says the correct size is in the owner's manual or on the Tire and Loading Information label on the driver's side door edge or post, and that you should buy the same size as the original tires or another size the vehicle maker recommends (NHTSA TireWise). USTMA adds that the placard also lists the spare size and the cold pressures for the front and rear axles, and says not to guess (USTMA Care and Service). The tires on the car now may not be the original size, so treat them as a cross check only. Our placard checklist lists what to write down.

Step 2: match these four codes on the sidewall

Using P225/60R16 97V as the example (full breakdown in how to read a tire size):

CodeExampleRule for the replacement
SizeP225/60R16Same size or a maker approved option; never smaller (USTMA)
Load index97 (1,609 lb per tire on Michelin's chart)Equal or higher; never less load capacity at placard pressure (Michelin, USTMA)
Speed ratingV (149 mph on Michelin's chart)Equal or higher than the vehicle maker specifies (USTMA)
ConstructionR (radial), P (passenger) or LTKeep all four the same size, speed rating and construction (USTMA)

USTMA also says a replacement must have an inflation pressure capability at least equal to the placard pressure, and that front and rear differences on the placard must be kept (USTMA). If a lower speed rated tire is fitted, USTMA says the vehicle's speed is restricted to that tire's rating and handling could change. If mixing speed ratings cannot be avoided, Michelin says the lower rated tires go on the front axle (Michelin). Load index and speed symbols are listed in our chart; XL tires are explained in XL vs SL.

Step 3: choose the tire type for your weather

With the codes fixed, the real choice is the tire type. NHTSA says all season tires handle a variety of road conditions with some mud and snow ability, winter tires are more effective in deep snow, summer tires are not designed for temperatures below freezing or for snow and ice, and all terrain tires are mainly for four wheel drive vehicles as an on road and off road compromise (NHTSA). For snow without a second set, Michelin says all weather tires carry the 3PMSF symbol, which requires a packed snow traction test, while M+S is based on tread geometry only (Michelin). Our which tire picker narrows the category by vehicle, climate and driving.

To compare tires within a type, NHTSA says the UTQG treadwear grade is relative to a control tire graded 100 (a 200 should wear twice as long under the test), and traction grades run AA, A, B, C for stopping on wet pavement (NHTSA). See our UTQG guide.

Step 4: decide how many to replace

  • Four: USTMA says replacing all four at the same time is recommended and preferred, and some vehicle makers advise against replacing fewer (USTMA).
  • Two: the new pair goes on the rear axle. USTMA says deeper tread grips better and clears water, and more grip at the rear on wet roads helps prevent oversteer (USTMA, Michelin).
  • One: USTMA says pair the new tire with the tire that has the deepest tread and put both on the rear axle (USTMA).
  • AWD and 4WD: USTMA says even small differences in outside diameter may cause drivetrain damage, so follow the owner's manual (USTMA). More in mixing tires.

Step 5: at the shop

  • Pressure: set to the placard, measured cold. NHTSA says the correct pressure is the vehicle maker's figure, not the number on the tire, and cold means not driven for at least three hours (NHTSA). See tire pressure.
  • Skip used tires: Michelin says you cannot know what damage a previously owned tire has had (Michelin).
  • Do not count the spare: NHTSA recommends that a full size spare not be used to replace worn tires except in emergencies such as a flat (NHTSA).
  • Check the date code on the new tires before they are mounted: how to read the DOT date code.

Frequently asked questions

How do I know what tire size my car needs?

NHTSA says to check the owner's manual or the Tire and Loading Information label on the driver's side door edge or post, and to buy the same size as the original tires or another size the vehicle maker recommends. If in doubt, ask the tire dealer.

Can I buy a different tire size?

Only an approved option. USTMA says replacement tires should be the original equipment size or approved options recommended by the vehicle or tire maker, and never a smaller size or one with less load carrying capacity at placard pressure. Michelin gives the same advice.

Is a higher load index OK?

Yes, equal or higher is allowed. Michelin says you can only mount a tire of equivalent or higher load than indicated for your vehicle. Lower is never OK, and pressure still comes from the placard.

Can I use a lower speed rating than the original tires?

It is not recommended. USTMA says the speed rating must be equal to or greater than the vehicle maker specifies to keep the vehicle's speed capability; with a lower rated tire, the vehicle's speed is restricted to that of the tire and handling may change.

Should I inflate new tires to the max psi on the sidewall?

No. NHTSA says the correct pressure is the one the vehicle maker lists on the door label or in the manual, not what is listed on the tire, measured cold (not driven for at least three hours).

If I replace only two tires, do they go on the front or the rear?

The rear. USTMA says two new tires of the same size and construction should go on the rear axle, because deeper tread grips better and clears water, which helps prevent oversteer on wet roads. Michelin says the same.

Can I replace just one tire on an AWD car?

Check the owner's manual first. USTMA says some vehicle makers advise against replacing fewer than four tires, and that on 4WD and AWD vehicles even small differences in outside diameter may cause drivetrain damage.

Are used tires a good way to save money?

Michelin advises avoiding used tires because you cannot know what damage or hard use a previously owned tire has had, and internal damage can lead to failure.
