Can you mix tires? When to replace 2 or 4

Short answer: replace all four when you can. If you replace two, match size, load index and speed rating, and put the new pair on the rear axle. Mixing brands is discouraged, and AWD needs closely matched diameters.

Last updated: October 13, 2026

By Smart Tire Picks Editorial Team

This page is for general information only and is not professional, safety, or legal advice. Always confirm tire size, load index, speed rating, and other specs against your vehicle’s door placard and owner’s manual, and have tires installed by a licensed professional. Check your tire placard and owner’s manual first. The door jamb placard and your owner’s manual give the size, load index, speed rating and cold inflation pressure set for your vehicle. If anything on this page differs, follow the vehicle maker, and not this site.

Short answer: the US Tire Manufacturers Association (USTMA) recommends replacing all four tires at once when you can. If you replace only two, match the size, load index and speed rating on the door placard, and put the new pair on the rear axle, not the drive axle alone. Mixing brands or models is discouraged. On AWD and 4WD, keep rolling diameters closely matched or you can stress the drivetrain.

Replace four when you can

USTMA’s replacing tires page says replacing all four at the same time is best for performance. A matched set keeps grip, braking feel and electronic aids more consistent left to right and front to rear. Toyo’s selection bulletin says the same idea in practice: unless the vehicle maker specifies otherwise, all road tires should share the same size, type and speed rating, and you should not mix different speed ratings on the vehicle.

Start from your door placard. Confirm size, then make sure the load index and speed rating meet or exceed the original. For how worn is too worn, see when to replace tires and tread depth.

Replacing two tires

Budget sometimes means a pair, not a full set. USTMA’s rules for replacing fewer than four:

  • Match the vehicle maker’s size, load index and speed rating.
  • Install the two new tires on the rear axle.

Why the rear? Newer tires clear water better. USTMA says greater rear traction helps avoid hydroplaning and an oversteer slide that can cost vehicle stability. BFGoodrich (and Michelin guidance quoted on that page) makes the same point: deeper tread on the rear reduces the chance of a fishtail in the wet, and rear traction loss is harder to recover from than front traction loss. Put the new pair on the rear even on a front wheel drive car.

After the install, keep rotating on schedule so wear stays even. See our tire rotation patterns guide.

Replacing one tire

USTMA warns that replacing a single tire can negatively affect suspension, transmission and treadwear. BFGoodrich adds that a lone new tire next to three worn ones changes rolling circumference, which hurts handling and can damage AWD drivetrains. If one tire must be replaced alone:

  • Pair the new tire with the tire that has the deepest remaining tread (USTMA says include a matching full size spare when choosing that partner).
  • Install that matched pair on the rear axle.

If the other three are already near the wear bars, replacing a pair or all four is usually the safer path. Have a licensed installer measure tread and advise.

Mixing brands and models

BFGoodrich says mixing different brands is not recommended. Compounds, tread patterns and construction differ, so braking and cornering responses can differ when you need them most. If you must mix for cost:

  • Keep a matched pair on each axle (same brand and model left and right).
  • Put the newer or deeper pair on the rear.
  • Do not mix radial tires with bias ply tires. All four must share the same construction.

Toyo separately advises against mixing different speed ratings on any vehicle.

Mixing tire sizes

Tires on the same axle must be the same size. Mixing sizes front to rear is only appropriate when the vehicle shipped with a staggered fitment from the factory. Otherwise use one size all around, taken from the placard. You can compare a proposed size change with our tire size comparison calculator, and still confirm load, speed rating, rim width and clearance with an installer.

AWD and 4WD special care

All wheel and four wheel drive systems often rely on all four tires having nearly the same rolling diameter. Toyo’s bulletin says 4WD and AWD need closely matched diameters to avoid strain on differentials or viscous couplings, and that inflation must follow the placard because pressure also changes circumference. BFGoodrich says that if the owner’s manual gives no mixing instructions, do not mix sizes on those vehicles at all.

Many AWD makers publish a maximum tread depth difference across the set (sometimes only a few 32nds of an inch). Check your owner’s manual before replacing two on an AWD vehicle; some require all four.

Winter tires are always four

Toyo says winter tires should always be fitted in matched sets of four. Fitting only two winter tires on the drive axle can create a large grip mismatch. Details on when to switch and why four matter are in our winter tires guide.

Buy and install checklist

  1. Copy size, load index, speed rating and cold PSI from the door placard.
  2. Prefer four matching tires. If buying two, plan them for the rear axle.
  3. Match construction (radial with radial). Avoid mixing brands when you can.
  4. On AWD or 4WD, confirm the maker’s tread depth difference limit before buying only two.
  5. Have a licensed installer mount, balance and torque the wheels, then set cold placard pressures and handle any TPMS relearn.

FAQ

Should I replace 2 tires or all 4?

USTMA recommends replacing all four at the same time for optimal performance. If that is not feasible, replace two that match the vehicle maker’s size, load index and speed rating, and install the new pair on the rear axle.

Why do new tires go on the rear axle?

Newer tires grip better in the wet. USTMA and BFGoodrich both say putting the deeper tread on the rear helps avoid hydroplaning and an oversteer slide that is harder to recover from than front grip loss. This applies whether the car is front, rear or all wheel drive.

Can I mix different tire brands on my car?

It is not recommended. BFGoodrich explains that brands and models differ in compound, tread pattern and construction, so grip and emergency responses can differ. If budget forces a mix, keep matched pairs on each axle and put the newer pair on the rear.

Is it safe to replace only one tire?

USTMA says replacing a single tire can negatively affect suspension, transmission and treadwear. If it is unavoidable, pair the new tire with the tire that has the deepest remaining tread (including a matching full size spare) and put that pair on the rear axle.

Can I mix tire sizes front and rear?

Only if the vehicle came from the factory with staggered sizes. Otherwise no. On AWD and 4WD, if the owner’s manual gives no mixing instructions, BFGoodrich says do not mix sizes at all, because rolling circumference differences can stress drivetrain parts.

Do winter tires have to be a set of four?

Yes. Toyo’s selection bulletin says winter tires should always be fitted in matched sets of four. Transport Canada and Canadian industry guidance say the same. See our winter tires guide.
