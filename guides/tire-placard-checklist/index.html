What to copy from your tire placard before you buy

Copy the size and the cold pressures off the door label before you look at a single tire. NHTSA TireWise calls it the Tire and Loading Information label, and says the right pressure is the one the vehicle maker printed there, not the maximum molded on the tire.

Last updated: November 20, 2026

By Smart Tire Picks Editorial Team

This page is for general information only and is not professional, safety, or legal advice. Always confirm tire size, load index, speed rating, and other specs against your vehicle's door placard and owner's manual, and have tires installed by a licensed professional. We have not lab tested products. See our Disclaimer.

Check your tire placard and owner's manual first. The door jamb placard and your owner's manual give the size, load index, speed rating and cold inflation pressure set for your vehicle. If anything on this page differs, follow the vehicle maker, and not this site.

Where the label actually is

NHTSA TireWise says the recommended pressure, in kilopascals and in pounds per square inch, is on the Tire and Loading Information label on the driver's side door edge or in the owner's manual. On a new vehicle the label is on the driver's side door jamb, which NHTSA calls the B pillar. If the vehicle has no B pillar, look at the rear edge of the driver's door. If that edge is too narrow, the label may be on an inward facing surface next to the driver's seat. Checked on the TireWise page, 20 November 2026.

That is a different sticker from the certification label, which carries the gross vehicle weight rating. Our placard guide walks the lines. This page is the copy list you take to the shop.

The copy list

  1. Vehicle. Year, make, model, and trim. A different trim can leave the factory on a different size.
  2. Size, exactly. Copy the size string character by character. If the label lists a front size and a rear size, copy both. NHTSA TireWise says to buy the original size or another size the vehicle manufacturer recommends, and to read that size on the label or in the manual.
  3. Cold pressure, front and rear. They are often different. The number on the tire sidewall is the most the tire may hold, not the pressure for your car. NHTSA winter driving tips says the same thing for winter: as the outside temperature drops, so does inflation pressure, and you still use the door label.
  4. What cold means. NHTSA TireWise says a cold tire has not been driven for at least three hours. Check every tire, including the spare, at least once a month. Keep a gauge in the car. A monthly cold pressure check is the habit. The tire pressure monitoring system only warns when a tire is significantly underinflated, and it is not a substitute for the gauge.
  5. Spare line. Temporary spares often have their own size and a different pressure, and a speed and distance limit printed on the spare or in the manual. Write those down too.
  6. Load index and speed rating. Many pressure labels show size and cold pressure, and not the full service description. Read the load index and speed symbol off a correct original tire, or from the owner's manual, and require the replacement to meet them. See load index and speed rating.
  7. What is on the car today. If it does not match the label, trust the label and the manual. A previous owner may have mounted a different size. Matching the tires that happen to be on the car is not the same as matching the vehicle.

If you are already on the road and a tire looks low

NHTSA TireWise says to fill it to the recommended cold pressure on the placard anyway, then check again when the tire is actually cold. A warm tire reads higher, so a warm reading that matches the cold number is still a little low. NHTSA's point is that driving a bit under the cold number is safer than driving a tire that is significantly underinflated. Do not bleed air from a hot tire to hit the cold number.

After you shop

The USTMA Tire Care and Safety Guide says the preferred replacement is all four tires at once. If you replace two, match size, construction, load index, and speed rating, and put the newer pair on the rear axle. If you can replace only one, pair that new tire with the tire that still has the deepest tread, and put both on the rear. The guide says a single new tire can affect suspension, gearing, the transmission, and how the tires wear. Read mixing tires before you buy a pair for an all wheel drive car.

Ask for the week and year in the DOT code on the set you will actually receive. Age is a separate question from tread depth. NHTSA TireWise says some vehicle and tire manufacturers recommend replacing tires at six to 10 years old regardless of remaining tread. The last four digits of the DOT code are the week and the year. Our DOT date code guide shows where to look, including the side of the tire that is hidden.

Frequently asked questions

What should I write down from the placard before shopping?

The size string, front and rear cold pressures, and any spare size or spare pressure. Also copy the load index and speed rating from a correct original tire or from the owner's manual, because the pressure label often does not print them.

Where is the label if it is not on the door edge?

NHTSA says a new vehicle carries it on the driver's side door jamb, the B pillar. With no B pillar, check the rear edge of the driver's door. If that edge is too narrow, look on an inward facing surface next to the driver's seat. The owner's manual is the other place NHTSA names.

What does cold pressure mean?

NHTSA says a cold tire has not been driven for at least three hours. Check all four tires and the spare. The tire pressure light only comes on when a tire is significantly underinflated.

The tires on the car do not match the label. Which do I follow?

Follow the label and the owner's manual. NHTSA says to buy the original size or another size the vehicle manufacturer recommends. Tires a previous owner installed are not that recommendation.

Is the placard enough by itself?

It is the starting point for size and cold pressure. Still read the manual for trim notes, and have a licensed installer confirm fitment, the tire pressure sensors, and the mount.
