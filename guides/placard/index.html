How to read your tire door placard

Your door jamb placard is the primary reference for original tire size and cold inflation pressure. Start here before you shop, inflate, or fit a spare.

This page is for general information only and is not professional, safety, or legal advice. Always confirm tire size, load index, speed rating, and other specs against your vehicle’s door placard and owner’s manual, and have tires installed by a licensed professional. Where to find it

On most passenger vehicles sold in the US, the tire information placard is on the driver’s door jamb (B-pillar area). Some vehicles also list tire data in the owner’s manual, the glove box door, or the fuel filler door. If labels conflict, follow the vehicle manufacturer’s guidance and ask a qualified technician.

Reading the placard line by line

US passenger vehicle labels are usually titled Tire and Loading Information. Layouts vary by maker, but most show the same items:

  • Seating capacity. The total number of seats, often split into front and rear.
  • Combined weight of occupants and cargo. A sentence such as “should never exceed XXX kg or XXX lbs.” This is the most people plus luggage the vehicle is rated to carry. It is a useful reminder that load index matters on replacement tires.
  • Original tire size. Front, rear, and sometimes spare, for example 225/65R17. Front and rear can differ on staggered setups. See how to choose tire size to decode the numbers.
  • Cold tire pressure. Recommended inflation for front, rear, and spare, usually shown in both kPa and PSI. “Cold” means checked before driving or after the car has sat for a few hours. The cold tire pressure guide covers how and when to check.

Some trims or load conditions have extra pressure notes in the owner’s manual (for example, a higher setting for full loads or towing). When the manual adds detail, it applies alongside the placard.

The spare tire line

If your vehicle shipped with a spare, the placard often gives it its own size and cold pressure. A compact temporary spare (sizes often start with a T, such as T125/70D17) commonly calls for a much higher PSI than the road tires. That is normal for its construction and not a typo.

  • Inflate the spare to its own placard line or the pressure printed on the spare, not to the front or rear number.
  • If there is no spare line, the car may have shipped with an inflator kit. Check the owner’s manual before assuming a pressure.
  • Temporary spares usually carry speed and distance limits. Follow the label on the spare and the owner’s manual.
  • Expect the tire pressure warning light to behave differently while a spare is mounted. See spare tires and TPMS caveats.

For a short checklist on keeping the spare ready, see spare PSI vs road (placard) PSI.

Placard vs the certification label

Many door jambs carry two stickers. The tire placard covers seating, the occupant and cargo limit, tire sizes, and cold pressures. The separate certification label usually lists the VIN, build month, and weight ratings such as gross vehicle weight rating (GVWR) and gross axle weight ratings. Both matter when choosing tires, but only the tire placard gives inflation pressures.

Why pressure on the placard is not the sidewall max

The large PSI number molded on a tire sidewall is a maximum cold pressure for the tire’s construction, not the recommended operating pressure for your vehicle. Use the placard (or OEM manual) values unless a manufacturer service bulletin says otherwise.

If the placard is missing or unreadable

  1. Check the tire section of the owner’s manual, including any online version from the vehicle maker.
  2. Ask a dealer service department to look up original tire size and pressures from the VIN.
  3. Do not treat the tires currently mounted as proof of the original size. A previous owner may have changed them.

Before you change sizes

Plus sizing or alternate sizes can affect speedometer accuracy, load capacity, clearance, and driver assistance calibration. Confirm compatibility with OEM documentation and a licensed installer. Do not rely solely on an online size picker.

Also verify load index and speed rating meet or exceed OEM requirements for your trim and options package. Copy everything into the placard checklist before you shop.

FAQ

Where is the tire information placard on most US vehicles?

On many passenger vehicles it is on the driver’s door jamb (B-pillar area). Some models also list tire data in the owner’s manual. If labels differ, follow the vehicle manufacturer’s guidance and ask a qualified technician.

Should I inflate to the big PSI number on the tire sidewall?

No. The sidewall figure is typically a maximum cold pressure for the tire’s construction. Use the vehicle placard (or OEM manual) recommended cold pressure unless a manufacturer service document says otherwise.

Can I change tire size from what the placard lists?

Alternate sizes can affect load capacity, clearance, speedometer accuracy, and related systems. Confirm compatibility with OEM documentation and a licensed installer rather than relying only on an online picker.

What does the spare line on the door placard mean?

When a vehicle ships with a spare, the placard often lists the spare size and a separate recommended cold pressure for it. A compact temporary spare commonly calls for a much higher PSI than the road tires. Use the spare’s own line, not the front or rear number. See spare PSI vs road PSI for checks and a short checklist.

My placard has no spare line. What should I use?

Some vehicles ship with an inflator kit instead of a spare, and some labels simply omit it. Check the owner’s manual and the spare tire sidewall, and ask a qualified technician if the sources disagree. Do not guess by copying the road tire pressure onto a compact spare.

Is the tire placard the same as the certification label with the VIN?

Not exactly. The Tire and Loading Information placard covers seating, the combined occupant and cargo limit, original tire sizes, and cold pressures. The separate certification label usually shows the VIN, build date, and weight ratings such as GVWR. On many vehicles the two sit next to each other on the door jamb.

What if my placard is missing, painted over, or unreadable?

Use the owner’s manual tire section first. A dealer service department can often look up the original tire size and pressures from the VIN. Avoid relying on the tires currently mounted, since a previous owner may have changed sizes.
