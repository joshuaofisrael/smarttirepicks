All season vs winter vs summer: winter tires win in deep snow

NHTSA TireWise sorts the choice by the weather you actually drive in. All season tires handle a variety of roads and have some mud and snow capability. Winter tires are more effective than all season tires in deep snow. Summer tires are warm weather tires, and they are not designed to operate below freezing or on snow and ice.

The NHTSA split, in one place

That three way split is the whole decision for most cars, and it is narrower than the marketing. "Some mud and snow capability" is what NHTSA grants all season tires. It does not say they match a winter tire in deep snow. The winter page, NHTSA winter driving tips, tells drivers to consider snow tires and then sends them to the tire safety ratings. It also says to keep at least 2/32 inch of tread and to set pressure from the door label, because pressure falls when the temperature falls.

There is a fourth name people shop for, all weather. Those tires are the ones that carry the three peak mountain snowflake, which is a packed snow test, not a new name for all season. The difference is on our all weather vs all season page. If you want one set and you do see snow, start there rather than assuming an all season tire is enough.

All season

This is the one set compromise for temperate driving: dry, wet, and light winter use, with the mud and snow capability NHTSA describes and no claim of deep snow equality. Confirm the size on the placard checklist before you compare models. If the winter where you live is more than a few storms, do not stop the comparison here.

Winter

Choose these when deep snow or ice is a normal part of the season, and plan on a second set for the warm months. The USTMA Tire Care and Safety Guide says that if winter tires go on the front axle they must also go on the rear, and that this applies to front wheel drive, four wheel drive, and all wheel drive. Do not put a winter pair on the drive axle and leave all season tires on the other axle. When the off season set comes off, store it as in our seasonal storage guide. Studs, the ice grip symbol, and the snowflake are different marks. The symbols and the storage calendar are in the winter tire guide.

Summer

NHTSA's line is the one to remember: not below freezing, and not on snow or ice. A summer tire can be the right tire where winter never arrives, and a poor one the week the temperature crosses freezing. If the car is a performance car that left the factory on summer tires, the cold limit does not go away because the car is quick. See which tire for a sports car.

Frequently asked questions

Are all season tires winter tires?

No. NHTSA says all season tires have some mud and snow capability, and that winter tires are more effective in deep snow. They are not the same category.

Can I drive summer tires in snow or below freezing?

NHTSA says summer tires are not designed to operate in temperatures below freezing, or on snow and ice.

Can I put winter tires on the front only?

No. USTMA says if winter tires are applied to the front axle they must also be installed on the rear. That includes front wheel drive, four wheel drive, and all wheel drive.

Is an all weather tire an all season tire?

Not in the way the snowflake is used. All weather tires carry the three peak mountain snowflake from a packed snow test. The comparison is on our all weather vs all season page.
