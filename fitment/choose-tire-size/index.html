How to choose tire size: copy the placard, not the old sidewall

NHTSA TireWise says that to keep the tire safe you buy the same size as the vehicle's original tires, or another size the vehicle manufacturer recommends. The place to read it is the owner's manual or the Tire and Loading Information label on the driver's side door edge or post. The tires already on the car are not that document.

Last updated: November 20, 2026

By Smart Tire Picks Editorial Team

This page is for general information only and is not professional, safety, or legal advice. Always confirm tire size, load index, speed rating, and other specs against your vehicle's door placard and owner's manual, and have tires installed by a licensed professional. We have not lab tested products. See our Disclaimer.

Check your tire placard and owner's manual first. The door jamb placard and your owner's manual give the size, load index, speed rating and cold inflation pressure set for your vehicle. If anything on this page differs, follow the vehicle maker, and not this site.

Step 1. Read the label, not the tire that is on the car

Write down the size and the cold pressures. Our placard checklist is the copy list, and the placard guide is the line by line reading. If you are unsure, NHTSA TireWise says a tire dealer can look up the correct size. That lookup is still checked against the label, not against a guess.

NHTSA TireWise also says the correct pressure is the one the vehicle manufacturer lists, not the number on the tire itself. Size and pressure are a pair. Copy both.

Step 2. Decode the sidewall only to audit it

Compare the size molded on today's tires with the label. A previous owner may have changed it. If they differ, the label wins. Matching "what is already mounted" is how the wrong size gets bought again.

Step 3. Load index and speed rating still have to pass

A size match with a lower load index is not a match. Read load index and speed rating and, if the placard or the original tire says extra load, XL versus SL. The USTMA Tire Care and Safety Guide says replacement tires should be the same size, load index, and speed rating, and that if the placard or the manual specifies a speed rated tire, the replacement speed rating must be the same or higher to keep the vehicle's speed capability. A speed rating is not permission to drive at that speed.

Step 4. All wheel drive is stricter than a size string

The USTMA Tire Care and Safety Guide says that if the owner's manual gives no mixing instructions for a four wheel drive or all wheel drive vehicle, do not mix tire sizes. All four tires must be marked with the same size, unless the vehicle manufacturer says otherwise. That includes a dedicated winter set. Do not mix tread pattern types, such as all terrain and all season, on the same vehicle. The guide's note is blunt: even small differences in outside diameter may cause drivetrain damage or mechanical malfunction, and some manufacturers advise against replacing fewer than four tires. Read the manual before you buy one tire. More on the rear axle rule is in mixing tires.

Step 5. Category, then the mount

Size does not pick the tread. NHTSA TireWise says all season tires handle a variety of roads and have some mud and snow capability, winter tires are more effective than all season tires in deep snow, and summer tires are not designed for temperatures below freezing or for snow and ice. The comparison is all season vs winter vs summer. The which tire picker asks climate and driving after the size is fixed.

Plan on mounting, balancing, and tire pressure sensor service. NHTSA TireWise says new tires should be balanced when they are installed, and that alignment extends tire life. If you are considering a larger wheel, treat it as a different size, not a style choice. Read plus sizing and speedometer error before you change the diameter. A taller tire makes the speedometer read low.

Frequently asked questions

Can I buy the size that is already on the car?

Only if it matches the door label or another size the vehicle manufacturer recommends. NHTSA says to read the original size on the label or in the owner's manual. A previous size change is not a recommendation.

Do all four tires have to be the same size?

USTMA recommends the same size, load index, and speed rating on all four, unless the vehicle manufacturer specifies different front and rear sizes. On four wheel drive and all wheel drive, if the manual is silent, do not mix sizes, including on a winter set.

Does the pressure on the sidewall tell me the right size pressure?

No. NHTSA says the vehicle manufacturer's pressure on the door label is the right one. The sidewall number is the most that tire may hold.
