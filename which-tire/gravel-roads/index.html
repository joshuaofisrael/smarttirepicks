Which tire should I get for gravel and dirt roads?

For a graded gravel road or dirt driveway, a highway all season tire is usually enough. If you drive loose dirt, sand, mud or rocks often, an all terrain tire is the better tool, and LT construction adds toughness.

Last updated: November 21, 2026

By Smart Tire Picks Editorial Team

This page is for general information only and is not professional, safety, or legal advice. Always confirm tire size, load index, speed rating, and other specs against your vehicle’s door placard and owner’s manual, and have tires installed by a licensed professional. We have not lab tested products. See our Disclaimer.

The short answer

Tire Rack says highway all season tires are not built for serious off roading but handle a dirt driveway or a two track down to a boat ramp. All terrain tires are built to dig through loose dirt and sand, mud and rocky terrain, with more noise and a little less wet grip on pavement (Tire Rack).

All four treads have to match on all wheel drive

Checked again 21 November 2026. For four wheel drive and all wheel drive, USTMA says that if the owner's manual is silent on mixing, do not mix tire sizes, do not mix radial and non radial construction, and do not mix tread pattern types such as all terrain and all season. The same guide says even small differences in outside diameter can damage the drivetrain. A gravel tire and a highway tire should not share one all wheel drive vehicle. See mixing tires before you replace only the pair that looks worn.

How to choose

  1. Mostly pavement, some graded gravel: highway all season or crossover touring all season.
  2. Regular rough or loose roads: on road or off road all terrain. Tire Rack lists five all terrain subcategories, from on road all terrain to max traction "mud tires" (Tire Rack).
  3. Sharp rock and puncture risk: Tire Rack says LT tires' thicker plies and reinforced sidewalls increase durability and puncture resistance (Tire Rack). Check fitment first; see LT vs P metric.

What to avoid

  • Mixing tread types on 4WD or AWD. USTMA says do not mix all terrain and all season tread (USTMA).
  • LT tires at passenger tire pressures. USTMA says switching to LT tires requires a significant pressure adjustment (USTMA).
  • Assuming all terrain means good on snow and ice. In Tire Rack's test the highway tire had better packed snow traction, and there was no real difference on ice (Tire Rack).

Size, load and speed checks before you buy

  • Size: USTMA says replacement tires should be the original equipment size or an approved option from the vehicle or tire maker, and never smaller or with less load capacity than the original size at placard pressure (USTMA Care and Service).
  • Load index: match or exceed the load capacity on the placard. See load index and speed rating and XL vs SL.
  • Speed rating: USTMA says the replacement speed rating must be equal to or greater than what the vehicle maker specifies to keep the vehicle's speed capability; a lower rated tire limits the vehicle to that tire's speed (USTMA).
  • All four alike: USTMA recommends all four tires share size, load index, speed rating and construction, and says never mix passenger tires with light truck tires on one vehicle (USTMA).

Frequently asked questions

Do I need all terrain tires for a gravel road?

Not for a well graded gravel road. Tire Rack says highway all season tires handle dirt driveways and two tracks; all terrain tires are for loose dirt, sand, mud and rocks.

Are LT tires better on gravel?

Tire Rack says LT construction increases durability and puncture resistance. It can also make the ride firmer and needs higher pressure, so confirm fitment with USTMA's P to LT guidance.

Are all terrain tires good in snow?

Some carry the 3PMSF symbol. In Tire Rack's 2015 test, the highway all season tire had better packed snow traction than the all terrain tire, and they were about equal on ice.
