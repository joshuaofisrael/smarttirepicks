Which tire should I get for towing?

For towing, the tire category matters less than load capacity. Use the size, load index and load range your vehicle maker specifies (often an LT tire on trucks), set pressure from the owner's manual for towing, and keep the trailer on its own correct tires.

Last updated: November 16, 2026

By Smart Tire Picks Editorial Team

This page is for general information only and is not professional, safety, or legal advice. Always confirm tire size, load index, speed rating, and other specs against your vehicle’s door placard and owner’s manual, and have tires installed by a licensed professional. We have not lab tested products. See our Disclaimer.

The short answer

Start with the vehicle maker's tow rating and tire spec. NHTSA says manufacturers' tow ratings can guide the choice of tow vehicle tires, and that tow vehicle tires may need higher pressure for towing, especially with heavy loads (NHTSA towing guide). If your truck came on LT tires, stay with LT tires in at least the same load range; USTMA advises against replacing original LT tires with passenger tires unless the vehicle maker allows it (USTMA).

How to choose

  1. Load capacity first. USTMA says replacements must have load capacity equal to or greater than the original tire at placard pressure (USTMA).
  2. LT construction for heavy work. Tire Rack says LT tires use thicker plies and reinforced sidewalls, suiting heavy hauling and towing, in load ranges C through F (Tire Rack).
  3. Load range on the sidewall. USTMA says the load range (for example Load Range E) identifies the tire's load and inflation limits (USTMA). See LT vs P metric for pressures by load range.
  4. Tread category second. Highway all season for paved towing, all terrain if you launch boats on gravel or work off road. See pickup truck tires.

Pressure and checks

  • NHTSA says underinflation reduces load capacity, may cause sway and control problems, and can lead to overheating and blowouts (NHTSA).
  • USTMA says to check pressure before towing a trailer, before long trips and before carrying heavy loads (USTMA).
  • Never exceed the maximum pressure on the sidewall.

What to avoid

  • Passenger tires at their full rating on a truck. USTMA divides passenger tire load by 1.10 on light trucks (USTMA).
  • Mismatched trailer tires. NHTSA says trailer tires should all be the same type, size and construction, in the size and load range on the trailer's label (NHTSA). See ST trailer tires.
  • Mixing LT and passenger tires on the tow vehicle (USTMA).

Size, load and speed checks before you buy

  • Size: USTMA says replacement tires should be the original equipment size or an approved option from the vehicle or tire maker, and never smaller or with less load capacity than the original size at placard pressure (USTMA Care and Service).
  • Load index: match or exceed the load capacity on the placard. See load index and speed rating and XL vs SL.
  • Speed rating: USTMA says the replacement speed rating must be equal to or greater than what the vehicle maker specifies to keep the vehicle's speed capability; a lower rated tire limits the vehicle to that tire's speed (USTMA).
  • All four alike: USTMA recommends all four tires share size, load index, speed rating and construction, and says never mix passenger tires with light truck tires on one vehicle (USTMA).

Frequently asked questions

Do I need LT tires to tow?

Use what the vehicle maker specifies. If the truck came with LT tires, stay with LT tires in at least the same load range. Some lighter vehicles tow on passenger tires within their tow rating; check the owner's manual.

Should I raise tire pressure when towing?

NHTSA says tow vehicle tires may require higher pressure for towing, especially heavy loads. Use the towing pressure in the owner's manual and never exceed the sidewall maximum.

What load range do I need for towing?

At least the load capacity of the original tires at placard pressure. Load range E is common on heavier pickups, but the right answer is on your placard and in the owner's manual.

Can I use LT tires on my trailer?

Follow the trailer's certification label. NHTSA says to buy the size, type and load range shown there. USTMA says ST tires should not be replaced with any other type.
