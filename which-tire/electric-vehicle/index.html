Which tire should I get for my electric vehicle?

For an EV, start with load: if the placard or original tires say HL, the replacement must be HL. After that, look for low rolling resistance for range and quiet construction, in the season category that suits your climate.

Last updated: November 16, 2026

By Smart Tire Picks Editorial Team

This page is for general information only and is not professional, safety, or legal advice. Always confirm tire size, load index, speed rating, and other specs against your vehicle’s door placard and owner’s manual, and have tires installed by a licensed professional. We have not lab tested products. See our Disclaimer.

The short answer

An EV tire is mostly about load capacity, then rolling resistance and noise. Michelin says if your EV came from the factory with HL tires, every replacement must also be HL rated, because HL tires carry more weight than XL tires at the same pressure (Michelin HL guide). Continental says lower rolling resistance means more range and that quiet tires matter more without engine noise (Continental). Choose all season, all weather or winter the same way you would for any car.

What HL means

USTMA's bulletin on High Load Capacity tires gives an example in one size: 245/40R19 94Y standard load carries 1477 lb at 36 psi, the XL version 98Y carries 1653 lb at 42 psi, and HL245/40R19 101Y carries 1819 lb at the same 42 psi (USTMA TISB 54). USTMA recommends replacing an HL tire with an HL tire of equal size and load capacity and notes that approved rim widths may differ. Michelin adds that an HL tire can replace an XL tire to add capacity (Michelin).

How to choose

  1. Copy size, load index and speed rating from the placard; check for an HL prefix on the current tires.
  2. Pick the season category for your climate (see the picker).
  3. Prefer low rolling resistance for range, which Continental calls essential for EVs (Continental).
  4. Consider noise. Some tires use foam inside the tread to reduce cabin noise; Continental describes this for its own line (Continental).
  5. Plan for wear. Michelin says that on average EVs wear tires about 20 percent faster than comparable gas cars, because of weight, torque and regenerative braking (Michelin EV FAQ). Continental recommends checking alignment every 6 months or after a hard curb or pothole hit (Continental).

What to avoid

  • Standard load or XL where HL is specified. Michelin says that runs the tire beyond its rated load, raising heat, wear and failure risk (Michelin).
  • Buying on size alone. A tire in the right size but a lower load index does not meet the requirement.
  • Skipping rotations. Faster wear makes regular rotation and pressure checks more important.

Size, load and speed checks before you buy

  • Size: USTMA says replacement tires should be the original equipment size or an approved option from the vehicle or tire maker, and never smaller or with less load capacity than the original size at placard pressure (USTMA Care and Service).
  • Load index: match or exceed the load capacity on the placard. See load index and speed rating and XL vs SL.
  • Speed rating: USTMA says the replacement speed rating must be equal to or greater than what the vehicle maker specifies to keep the vehicle's speed capability; a lower rated tire limits the vehicle to that tire's speed (USTMA).
  • All four alike: USTMA recommends all four tires share size, load index, speed rating and construction, and says never mix passenger tires with light truck tires on one vehicle (USTMA).

Frequently asked questions

Do EVs need special tires?

They need tires that meet the load index on the placard, which may mean HL or XL tires. Low rolling resistance and low noise help range and comfort. The season category still depends on your climate.

Can I put XL tires on an EV that came with HL tires?

No. Michelin says that if the vehicle came with HL tires, replacements must also be HL rated. USTMA recommends replacing HL with HL of equal size and load capacity.

Why do EV tires wear out faster?

Michelin says more weight, instant torque and regenerative braking combine to wear tires about 20 percent faster on average than a comparable gas vehicle.

Do low rolling resistance tires increase EV range?

Continental says lower rolling resistance means increased electric range and higher efficiency. How much depends on the vehicle and driving.
