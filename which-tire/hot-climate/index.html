Which tire should I get for a hot climate like Florida?

Where it rarely gets cold, you do not need winter capability. A grand touring all season tire suits most drivers, and summer tires are an option in places that stay warm. Check the UTQG temperature grade and keep pressure at the placard value.

Last updated: November 16, 2026

By Smart Tire Picks Editorial Team

This page is for general information only and is not professional, safety, or legal advice. Always confirm tire size, load index, speed rating, and other specs against your vehicle’s door placard and owner’s manual, and have tires installed by a licensed professional. We have not lab tested products. See our Disclaimer.

The short answer

Michelin says that in climates that stay above freezing, summer tires can make sense even for everyday driving, since they are optimized for warm weather grip and wet roads above about 45°F (Michelin). Most commuters will still be happy with a touring all season tire for its comfort and tread life (Tire Rack). Frequent summer storms make wet grip worth prioritizing; see which tire for rain.

Heat: what the temperature grade means

UTQG temperature grades are A, B and C, measuring resistance to heat buildup on an indoor test wheel. Grade C is the federal minimum under FMVSS 109; A and B are higher (49 CFR 575.104). The rule warns that excessive speed, underinflation or excessive load can still cause heat buildup and possible tire failure. USTMA repeats that the grade assumes proper inflation and load (USTMA).

How to choose

  1. Commuting, comfort, tread life: grand or standard touring all season.
  2. Sporty car, always warm: max performance summer. See sports car tires.
  3. Compare temperature and traction grades on your shortlist.
  4. Check pressure cold. NHTSA says check tires when cold, after at least three hours parked, and inflate to the placard, not the sidewall maximum (NHTSA).

What to avoid

  • Underinflation in heat, which the UTQG warning links to heat buildup (49 CFR 575.104).
  • Overloading the vehicle beyond the placard limits.
  • Summer tires on a trip north in winter. Michelin says they should not be used near freezing (Michelin).

Size, load and speed checks before you buy

  • Size: USTMA says replacement tires should be the original equipment size or an approved option from the vehicle or tire maker, and never smaller or with less load capacity than the original size at placard pressure (USTMA Care and Service).
  • Load index: match or exceed the load capacity on the placard. See load index and speed rating and XL vs SL.
  • Speed rating: USTMA says the replacement speed rating must be equal to or greater than what the vehicle maker specifies to keep the vehicle's speed capability; a lower rated tire limits the vehicle to that tire's speed (USTMA).
  • All four alike: USTMA recommends all four tires share size, load index, speed rating and construction, and says never mix passenger tires with light truck tires on one vehicle (USTMA).

Frequently asked questions

Do I need all season tires in Florida?

You do not need winter capability. All season tires remain a sensible default for comfort and tread life, and Michelin says summer tires can be worth considering where it stays above freezing.

What UTQG temperature grade should I look for?

A is the highest, C is the federal minimum. A higher grade means more resistance to heat on the lab test, but the grade assumes correct inflation and load.

Does heat make tires wear faster?

We could not find a source we trust that gives a number, so we do not state one. What the federal grading text does say is that speed, underinflation and overload drive heat buildup, which can damage a tire.
