Which tire should I get for my pickup truck?

For a pickup that mostly drives paved roads, a highway all season tire is the usual match. Choose all terrain if you work or play off pavement, and an LT tire in the load range your truck specifies if you haul or tow heavy.

Last updated: November 16, 2026

By Smart Tire Picks Editorial Team

This page is for general information only and is not professional, safety, or legal advice. Always confirm tire size, load index, speed rating, and other specs against your vehicle’s door placard and owner’s manual, and have tires installed by a licensed professional. We have not lab tested products. See our Disclaimer.

The short answer

Match the tire to the hardest job your truck does regularly. Tire Rack groups truck tires from highway all season for paved commuting through on road all terrain up to max traction "mud" tires for serious off road use (Tire Rack truck tires). Then make sure the construction is right: if your truck came on LT tires, USTMA says not to replace them with passenger tires unless the vehicle maker says otherwise (USTMA).

How to choose

  1. Read the placard and the sidewall. An LT size such as LT245/75R16 Load Range E tells you it is a light truck tire, and USTMA says the load range identifies its load and inflation limits (USTMA). A P in front of the size means a passenger tire.
  2. Paved roads most days: highway all season. Tire Rack found it quieter and better in the wet on pavement than an all terrain tire (Tire Rack).
  3. Dirt roads, job sites, trails: all terrain. Tire Rack says all terrain tires are built to dig through loose dirt and sand, mud and rocky ground, at the cost of more tread noise, a little less responsive handling and a modest drop in wet traction on pavement (Tire Rack).
  4. Heavy hauling or towing: LT construction. Tire Rack says LT tires use thicker plies, reinforced sidewalls and heavier materials, and come in load ranges such as C, D, E and F (Tire Rack). See which tire for towing.

What to avoid

  • Putting passenger tires on a truck that came with LT. USTMA advises against it unless the vehicle maker specifies otherwise (USTMA).
  • Assuming a passenger tire carries its full rating on a truck. USTMA says passenger tire load capacity is divided by 1.10 on light trucks; its example P265/70R17 drops from 2535 lb to 2305 lb (USTMA).
  • Mixing. USTMA says never mix passenger and LT tires on one vehicle, and on 4WD do not mix all terrain with all season tread (USTMA).
  • Upsizing without checks. Larger tires need rim, load and clearance checks. See plus sizing.

Size, load and speed checks before you buy

  • Size: USTMA says replacement tires should be the original equipment size or an approved option from the vehicle or tire maker, and never smaller or with less load capacity than the original size at placard pressure (USTMA Care and Service).
  • Load index: match or exceed the load capacity on the placard. See load index and speed rating and XL vs SL.
  • Speed rating: USTMA says the replacement speed rating must be equal to or greater than what the vehicle maker specifies to keep the vehicle's speed capability; a lower rated tire limits the vehicle to that tire's speed (USTMA).
  • All four alike: USTMA recommends all four tires share size, load index, speed rating and construction, and says never mix passenger tires with light truck tires on one vehicle (USTMA).

Frequently asked questions

Are all terrain tires worth it on a pickup?

Only if you use the capability. Tire Rack's paved road test found the all terrain tire louder, slightly less precise and weaker in the wet than a highway all season tire, and it used about 3 percent more fuel in their test.

What does load range E mean?

It is a light truck load range. USTMA says the load range identifies the tire's load and inflation limits. Our LT vs P guide lists the inflation pressures for load ranges C, D and E.

Can I replace LT tires with P metric tires?

USTMA says it is not recommended to replace original LT tires with P metric or Euro metric tires unless the vehicle maker specifies it.

Do mud terrain tires work on the highway?

They are the most specialized end of the all terrain range, which Tire Rack calls max traction or mud tires. Expect the paved road trade offs of all terrain tires (noise, wet grip) to be larger, so pick them only for regular mud or rock use.
