Which tire should I get for a hybrid?

For a hybrid or plug in hybrid, start with the door placard: same size, a load index at least as high (XL or HL if that is what it lists) and a speed rating at least as high. Then pick a low rolling resistance tire in a touring all season or all weather tread for your climate, and keep it at placard pressure.

By Smart Tire Picks Editorial Team

Always confirm tire size, load index, speed rating, and other specs against your vehicle’s door placard and owner’s manual, and have tires installed by a licensed professional. The short answer

A hybrid needs the same basics as any car plus two priorities: low rolling resistance, because that is what protects the fuel economy you bought the car for, and enough load capacity, because the battery adds weight. NHTSA says low rolling resistance replacement tires improve fuel economy and extend the range of electric and hybrid vehicles, and that carmakers increasingly fit them from the factory (NHTSA TireWise). Michelin's plug in hybrid guide says if the vehicle specifies XL or HL tires, replacements must carry the same designation (Michelin plug in hybrid guide). Choose all season, all weather or winter by climate, as for any car.

Why rolling resistance matters on a hybrid

NHTSA estimates that a 10 percent reduction in tire rolling resistance gives a 1 to 2 percent improvement in fuel economy, and says low rolling resistance tires commonly have higher inflation pressure and reduced sidewall and tread deflection (NHTSA). Michelin says that on a plug in hybrid, lower rolling resistance stretches electric range and also improves mpg when the engine is running (Michelin).

Size matters too. Michelin says a wider tire generally improves dry grip but increases rolling resistance, and that the factory size is the vehicle maker's balance of those trade offs (Michelin tire size guide). On a hybrid that is one more reason to stay with the placard size rather than going wider; see plus sizing before changing it.

How to choose

  1. Copy the placard. Size, load index and speed rating, plus any XL or HL marking on the current tires. Michelin says HL tires are built for heavier vehicles such as electric vehicles, hybrids and SUVs, an HL tire must be replaced with HL, and HL can replace XL for extra capacity (Michelin HL guide).
  2. Check for an automaker marking on the sidewall of the original tires. Michelin says marked original equipment tires are engineered for the vehicle's handling, ride, noise and rolling resistance targets, and recommends replacing them with the same approved marking when you can (Michelin marked tires).
  3. Pick the tread for your climate: touring all season for most drivers, all weather with the 3PMSF symbol if you get snow (see snow without winter tires), dedicated winter for regular ice.
  4. Within that category, prefer low rolling resistance as described by the tire maker, and compare UTQG treadwear grades for tread life (UTQG guide).
  5. Consider noise. Michelin notes that plug in hybrids can be quieter than gas cars, so tire noise in the cabin can stand out if the tire is not designed for a quiet ride (Michelin).

Keep the savings: pressure and rotation

  • Pressure: FuelEconomy.gov says keeping tires at the right pressure improves gas mileage by 0.6 percent on average and up to 3 percent in some cases, that under inflation lowers mileage by about 0.2 percent for every 1 psi drop in the average pressure of all tires, and that the right number is on the door jamb sticker, not the sidewall maximum (FuelEconomy.gov). See tire pressure.
  • Rotation: Michelin says plug in hybrid tires wear faster than tires on equivalent gas cars because of battery weight and motor torque, and its guide suggests rotating every 6,000 to 8,000 miles or at the first sign of uneven wear, while following the vehicle maker's interval (Michelin). See tire rotation.

What to avoid

  • Trading load or speed rating for efficiency. A lower load index or speed rating than the placard does not meet the requirement, however efficient the tire is (USTMA).
  • Standard load where XL or HL is specified (Michelin, Michelin).
  • A wider size for looks, which Michelin says raises rolling resistance (Michelin).

Size, load and speed checks before you buy

  • Size: USTMA says replacement tires should be the original equipment size or an approved option from the vehicle or tire maker, and never smaller or with less load capacity than the original size at placard pressure (USTMA Care and Service).
  • Load index: match or exceed the load capacity on the placard. See load index and speed rating and XL vs SL.
  • Speed rating: USTMA says the replacement speed rating must be equal to or greater than what the vehicle maker specifies to keep the vehicle's speed capability; a lower rated tire limits the vehicle to that tire's speed (USTMA).
  • All four alike: USTMA recommends all four tires share size, load index, speed rating and construction, and says never mix passenger tires with light truck tires on one vehicle (USTMA).

Frequently asked questions

Do hybrids need special tires?

Not a separate tire type, but they do best on low rolling resistance tires in the placard size and load rating. NHTSA says low rolling resistance replacement tires improve fuel economy and extend the range of electric and hybrid vehicles.

How much fuel do low rolling resistance tires save?

NHTSA estimates that a 10 percent cut in rolling resistance improves fuel economy by 1 to 2 percent. Your result depends on the tire you are replacing and how you drive.

Do I need XL or HL tires on my hybrid?

Only if the placard or original tires call for them. Michelin says if the vehicle specifies XL or HL, replacements must carry that designation, an HL tire must be replaced with HL, and HL can replace XL.

Can I put regular all season tires on a hybrid?

Yes, if they match the placard size, load index and speed rating. Prefer a model the maker describes as low rolling resistance if fuel economy matters to you.

Does tire pressure affect hybrid mpg?

Yes. FuelEconomy.gov says under inflation lowers gas mileage by about 0.2 percent for every 1 psi drop in average pressure, and proper inflation improves mileage by 0.6 percent on average. Use the door jamb pressure, not the sidewall maximum.

Should I replace original equipment tires with the same model?

Where the original tires carry an automaker marking, Michelin recommends replacing them with the same approved marking when possible, because those tires are tuned for the vehicle's handling, noise and rolling resistance targets.
