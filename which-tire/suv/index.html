Which tire should I get for my SUV or crossover?

Most crossovers and SUVs that stay on pavement are best matched with a highway all season or crossover/SUV touring all season tire. Choose an all weather tire with the 3PMSF symbol if you get real snow, and all terrain only if you leave the pavement.

Last updated: November 16, 2026

By Smart Tire Picks Editorial Team

This page is for general information only and is not professional, safety, or legal advice. Always confirm tire size, load index, speed rating, and other specs against your vehicle’s door placard and owner’s manual, and have tires installed by a licensed professional. We have not lab tested products. See our Disclaimer.

The short answer

If your SUV or crossover spends nearly all its time on paved roads, start with the highway all season or crossover/SUV touring all season category. Tire Rack describes these as the categories for daily commuters who rarely leave pavement, tuned for a quiet, comfortable ride, and offered in both all season and all weather versions (Tire Rack truck and SUV tires). If winter brings packed snow, look for an all weather tire with the three peak mountain snowflake (3PMSF) symbol. Pick all terrain only if you regularly drive dirt, sand, mud or rocks.

How to choose

  1. Start at the door placard. It lists the original size, load and pressure. Copy it with our placard checklist.
  2. Decide where you drive. In Tire Rack's paved road comparison, the highway all season tire rode better and was quieter, while the all terrain tire had a steady growl above 20 mph, less wet grip and a longer wet braking distance (Tire Rack test).
  3. Decide how much winter you get. Michelin says all season tires carry M+S, which is based on tread design rather than a snow test, while 3PMSF tires have passed a standardized packed snow traction test (Michelin). For regular ice and deep cold, a dedicated winter tire is still the stronger choice.
  4. Rank your priorities. If quiet and comfort matter most, touring and highway all season tires are built for that. If long tread life matters, compare UTQG treadwear numbers on the tires you shortlist (see UTQG).

What to avoid

  • All terrain tires for looks only. You give up some quiet, wet grip and, in Tire Rack's test, about 3 percent fuel economy (22.7 vs 22.0 mpg) for capability you may not use (Tire Rack).
  • Switching to LT tires without checking. Many SUVs ship on passenger tires. USTMA says passenger tires can sometimes be replaced with light truck tires, but it takes a significant pressure change, rim and clearance checks, and handling may be affected (USTMA). See LT vs P metric.
  • Mixing tread types on AWD. USTMA says do not mix tread pattern types such as all terrain and all season on 4WD and AWD vehicles, and keep all four the same size (USTMA).

Size, load and speed checks before you buy

  • Size: USTMA says replacement tires should be the original equipment size or an approved option from the vehicle or tire maker, and never smaller or with less load capacity than the original size at placard pressure (USTMA Care and Service).
  • Load index: match or exceed the load capacity on the placard. See load index and speed rating and XL vs SL.
  • Speed rating: USTMA says the replacement speed rating must be equal to or greater than what the vehicle maker specifies to keep the vehicle's speed capability; a lower rated tire limits the vehicle to that tire's speed (USTMA).
  • All four alike: USTMA recommends all four tires share size, load index, speed rating and construction, and says never mix passenger tires with light truck tires on one vehicle (USTMA).

Frequently asked questions

Do I need all terrain tires on an AWD crossover?

Not for paved driving. AWD helps you accelerate but does not change which tire suits your roads. Tire Rack found highway all season tires quieter, smoother and grippier in the wet on pavement, while all terrain tires make sense when you regularly leave the pavement.

Are all weather tires good for an SUV?

If you get packed snow but do not want a second set, an all weather tire with the 3PMSF symbol is the category to look at. Michelin says the 3PMSF symbol means the tire passed a standardized packed snow traction test, but it does not cover pure ice.

Can I put LT tires on my SUV?

Sometimes. USTMA says it requires a significant inflation pressure adjustment to carry the same load, rim width and rim load checks, and clearance checks, and that handling may change. Ask the vehicle maker or a tire professional first, and never mix LT and passenger tires on one vehicle.

How do I know which load index my SUV needs?

Use the size and load index on the door placard or in the owner's manual. USTMA says never choose a replacement with less load carrying capacity than the original tire at placard pressure.
