Which tire should I get?

Tell us your vehicle, climate, driving and top priority, and the picker suggests a tire category with plain English reasons. Then use the guides below to check size, load index, speed rating and the snow symbol.

Five marks to check on any tire

  • Size and load index: never smaller or with less load capacity than the original at placard pressure (USTMA). Load index guide.
  • Speed rating: equal to or higher than the vehicle maker specifies (USTMA).
  • UTQG: treadwear, traction (AA to C, straight ahead wet braking only) and temperature (A to C) grades (49 CFR 575.104). UTQG guide.
  • 3PMSF vs M+S: 3PMSF requires a packed snow test; M+S does not (Michelin).
  • LT vs P, and HL or XL: never mix passenger and LT tires on one vehicle (USTMA); replace HL with HL (USTMA TISB 54). LT vs P guide.

Which tire for...

Which tire should I get for my SUV or crossover?

Most crossovers and SUVs that stay on pavement are best matched with a highway all season or crossover/SUV touring all season tire. Choose an all weather tire with the 3PMSF symbol if you get real snow, and all terrain only if you leave the pavement.

Which tire should I get for my pickup truck?

For a pickup that mostly drives paved roads, a highway all season tire is the usual match. Choose all terrain if you work or play off pavement, and an LT tire in the load range your truck specifies if you haul or tow heavy.

Read the guide →

Which tire should I get for my electric vehicle?

For an EV, start with load: if the placard or original tires say HL, the replacement must be HL. After that, look for low rolling resistance for range and quiet construction, in the season category that suits your climate.

Read the guide →

Which tire for snow if you don't want winter tires?

If you want one set of tires year round and still see snow, choose an all weather tire with the three peak mountain snowflake (3PMSF) symbol. An M+S marking alone does not mean the tire passed a snow test.

Read the guide →

Which tire should I get for highway commuting?

For a car that racks up highway miles, a touring all season tire is the usual match. Grand touring leans toward quiet, comfort and handling; standard touring leans toward long tread life.

Read the guide →

Which tire should I get for towing?

For towing, the tire category matters less than load capacity. Use the size, load index and load range your vehicle maker specifies (often an LT tire on trucks), set pressure from the owner's manual for towing, and keep the trailer on its own correct tires.

Read the guide →

Which tire should I get for my minivan?

Most minivans are best matched with a touring all season tire: standard touring if tread life and cost per mile matter most, grand touring if you want a quieter, more composed ride. Make sure the load index covers a fully loaded van.

Read the guide →

Which tire should I get for a sports car?

If it stays warm where you drive, a max performance summer tire gives the most grip. If you need one tire year round, an ultra high performance all season tire trades some grip for cold weather use. Below about 45°F, summer tires should come off.

Read the guide →

Which tire should I get for rain?

For wet roads, choose a category built for wet grip, compare the UTQG traction grade among your shortlist, and replace tires before tread runs low. Remember the traction grade measures straight ahead wet braking only.

Read the guide →

Which tire should I get for a hot climate like Florida?

Where it rarely gets cold, you do not need winter capability. A grand touring all season tire suits most drivers, and summer tires are an option in places that stay warm. Check the UTQG temperature grade and keep pressure at the placard value.

Read the guide →

Which tire should I get for gravel and dirt roads?

For a graded gravel road or dirt driveway, a highway all season tire is usually enough. If you drive loose dirt, sand, mud or rocks often, an all terrain tire is the better tool, and LT construction adds toughness.

Read the guide →

Which tire should I get for a Jeep Wrangler style 4x4?

If your 4x4 spends most of its miles on pavement with weekend trails, choose an all terrain tire. Pick mud terrain only if you regularly drive deep mud, loose soil or rock, and a highway all season if you rarely leave the road.

Read the guide →

Which tire should I get for a hybrid?

For a hybrid or plug in hybrid, start with the door placard: same size, a load index at least as high (XL or HL if that is what it lists) and a speed rating at least as high. Then pick a low rolling resistance tire in a touring all season or all weather tread for your climate, and keep it at placard pressure.

Frequently asked questions

Which tire should I buy for my car?

Start with your door placard for size, load index and speed rating. Then choose a category: for most cars that is a grand touring all season tire, standard touring if tread life matters most, a 3PMSF all weather or winter tire if you get snow, or summer tires for warm weather performance.

Does this picker recommend brands?

No. It recommends a tire category and what to check on the sidewall. We do not rank branded models unless we have a sourced review page.

What is the difference between all season and all weather tires?

Michelin says all season tires carry M+S, based on tread design, while all weather tires carry the 3PMSF symbol, which requires passing a packed snow traction test.

Do I need to replace all four tires at once?

USTMA says replacing all four at once is recommended and preferred, and on 4WD and AWD vehicles even small diameter differences can damage the drivetrain. See our mixing tires guide.
