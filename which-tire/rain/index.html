Which tire should I get for rain?

For wet roads, choose a category built for wet grip, compare the UTQG traction grade among your shortlist, and replace tires before tread runs low. Remember the traction grade measures straight ahead wet braking only.

Last updated: November 16, 2026

By Smart Tire Picks Editorial Team

This page is for general information only and is not professional, safety, or legal advice. Always confirm tire size, load index, speed rating, and other specs against your vehicle’s door placard and owner’s manual, and have tires installed by a licensed professional. We have not lab tested products. See our Disclaimer.

The short answer

Wet grip varies a lot by category. In Tire Rack's category test, max performance summer tires were the wet leaders, ultra high performance all season tires improved on touring tires in the wet, and the standard touring tires struggled for wet grip (Tire Rack). Tire Rack describes grand touring summer tires as emphasizing wet traction and hydroplaning resistance (Tire Rack). Whatever you choose, keep the tread healthy.

How to choose

  1. Compare the traction grade. Grades run AA, A, B, C, based on wet braking on government test surfaces; a C tire may have poor traction (49 CFR 575.104).
  2. Know its limits. The same rule warns the grade is based on straight ahead braking and does not include acceleration, cornering, hydroplaning or peak traction (49 CFR 575.104).
  3. Pick the category for your temperatures. Summer tires suit warm rain; below about 45°F choose all season, all weather or winter (Michelin).
  4. Watch tread depth. NHTSA says tread should be at least 2/32 inch on all tires (NHTSA). See tread depth and when to replace.

What to avoid

  • All terrain tires for a wet commute. Tire Rack's paved road test found a longer wet braking distance for the all terrain tire (Tire Rack).
  • Extreme performance tires in standing water. Tire Rack noted its extreme performance summer tire tended to hydroplane in standing water (Tire Rack).
  • Low pressure. Check monthly; see tire pressure.

Size, load and speed checks before you buy

  • Size: USTMA says replacement tires should be the original equipment size or an approved option from the vehicle or tire maker, and never smaller or with less load capacity than the original size at placard pressure (USTMA Care and Service).
  • Load index: match or exceed the load capacity on the placard. See load index and speed rating and XL vs SL.
  • Speed rating: USTMA says the replacement speed rating must be equal to or greater than what the vehicle maker specifies to keep the vehicle's speed capability; a lower rated tire limits the vehicle to that tire's speed (USTMA).
  • All four alike: USTMA recommends all four tires share size, load index, speed rating and construction, and says never mix passenger tires with light truck tires on one vehicle (USTMA).

Frequently asked questions

Does a traction AA tire stop best in the rain?

AA is the highest UTQG traction grade for straight ahead wet braking on the government test. It does not measure cornering or hydroplaning, so treat it as one clue.

Which tire category is best in the wet?

In Tire Rack's category test, max performance summer tires led in the wet, but they are for warm weather. For year round use, the ultra high performance all season tires improved on the touring tires in the wet.

When should I replace tires for rain?

NHTSA's minimum is 2/32 inch. Many drivers replace earlier for wet roads; see our tread depth guide for how to measure.
