Which tire should I get for a Jeep Wrangler style 4x4?

If your 4x4 spends most of its miles on pavement with weekend trails, choose an all terrain tire. Pick mud terrain only if you regularly drive deep mud, loose soil or rock, and a highway all season if you rarely leave the road.

Last updated: November 17, 2026

By Smart Tire Picks Editorial Team

This page is for general information only and is not professional, safety, or legal advice. Always confirm tire size, load index, speed rating, and other specs against your vehicle’s door placard and owner’s manual, and have tires installed by a licensed professional. We have not lab tested products. See our Disclaimer.

The short answer

Choose by where you actually drive, not by looks. BFGoodrich says all terrain tires give a quieter, more comfortable highway ride and better on road handling than mud terrain tires while still handling moderate trails, and that mud terrain tires are for deep mud, sand, loose soil and technical rock crawling, at the cost of noise, fuel economy and tread life (BFGoodrich tire selection). Its own advice: if most of your miles are highway with occasional trails, all terrain is the better tire even though mud terrain looks tougher. If your 4x4 rarely leaves pavement, a highway all season tire in the placard size is the quieter, simpler choice (see which tire for an SUV).

NHTSA describes all terrain tires as mainly used on four wheel drive vehicles and as a good compromise between on road driving and off road capability (NHTSA TireWise).

Snow: check the symbol, not the tread blocks

Many off road tires carry M+S. Michelin says M+S is based on tread geometry, not a performance test, while the three peak mountain snowflake (3PMSF) symbol requires passing a standardized traction test on medium packed snow, and it still does not cover pure ice (Michelin). If you drive in winter, look for 3PMSF on the all terrain sizes you shortlist, or run a dedicated winter tire.

LT or P, bigger sizes and pressure

  • Keep LT if it came with LT. BFGoodrich says never replace original LT tires with P metric tires because you lose load capacity and durability (BFGoodrich). USTMA says P to LT swaps are possible on some vehicles but need a rim and inflation check, clearance check and may change handling (USTMA). See LT vs P metric.
  • Going bigger is a vehicle change, not just a tire change. BFGoodrich says larger than stock tires often need a lift kit and changes to brake lines, steering and suspension, which can affect handling and rollover risk (BFGoodrich). USTMA says any size change needs clearance checks at all four wheels through full suspension travel and full lock, and that diameter changes can affect the speedometer, ABS, TPMS and 4WD systems (USTMA). See plus sizing and speedometer error.
  • New size, new pressure. BFGoodrich says the door placard pressures no longer apply after an upsize and to have a tire professional work out the right pressure (BFGoodrich). Air back up after airing down for a trail before you drive on pavement.
  • Do not mix tread types. USTMA and Michelin both say not to mix tread pattern types such as all terrain and all season on 4WD vehicles, and to keep all four the same size; Michelin says the outside circumferences should be within 1 inch of each other (USTMA, Michelin).
  • Match the spare. BFGoodrich suggests carrying a full size spare within about 1 inch of your main tire diameter (BFGoodrich), and a spare that matches the other four lets you rotate five (see tire rotation).

Size, load and speed checks before you buy

  • Size: USTMA says replacement tires should be the original equipment size or an approved option from the vehicle or tire maker, and never smaller or with less load capacity than the original size at placard pressure (USTMA Care and Service).
  • Load index: match or exceed the load capacity on the placard. See load index and speed rating and XL vs SL.
  • Speed rating: USTMA says the replacement speed rating must be equal to or greater than what the vehicle maker specifies to keep the vehicle's speed capability; a lower rated tire limits the vehicle to that tire's speed (USTMA).
  • All four alike: USTMA recommends all four tires share size, load index, speed rating and construction, and says never mix passenger tires with light truck tires on one vehicle (USTMA).

Frequently asked questions

Are mud terrain tires bad on the highway?

They work on the road but cost you comfort. BFGoodrich says mud terrain tires are not quiet or fuel efficient and trade road noise, on road grip and tread life for maximum bite in deep mud. If most of your miles are paved, it recommends all terrain instead.

Do I need all terrain tires on a Jeep that never goes off road?

No. NHTSA describes all terrain tires as a compromise between on road and off road use. If you never leave pavement, a highway all season tire in the placard size gives up nothing you use.

Are all terrain tires good in snow?

It depends on the tire. Look for the 3PMSF symbol, which Michelin says requires passing a packed snow traction test; M+S alone is based on tread design. Neither symbol covers pure ice.

Can I put bigger tires on my 4x4?

Only with the checks done. BFGoodrich says larger tires often require a lift and steering, brake line and suspension changes that can raise rollover risk, and USTMA says to check clearance at all four wheels and that a diameter change can affect the speedometer, ABS, TPMS and 4WD systems.

Can I replace just two tires on a 4x4?

Check the owner's manual first. USTMA says that for 4WD and AWD vehicles even small differences in outside diameter may cause drivetrain damage, and that replacing all four together is recommended and preferred.
