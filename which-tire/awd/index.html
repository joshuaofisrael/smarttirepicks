Which tire should I get for an AWD car?

Match all four tires before you argue about tread. On the 2023 Subaru Crosstrek manual opened for this page, circumference and degree of wear are their own requirements, separate from the size code on the sidewall.

Last updated: November 20, 2026

By Smart Tire Picks Editorial Team

This page is for general information only and is not professional, safety, or legal advice. Always confirm tire size, load index, speed rating, and other specs against your vehicle's door placard and owner's manual, and have tires installed by a licensed professional. We have not lab tested products. See our Disclaimer.

The short answer

An all wheel drive car does not get a special tread the way a truck gets a load range. It gets a matching problem. The 2023 Subaru Crosstrek and Crosstrek Wilderness owner's manual says that when you replace or install tires, all four must be the same in eight things: size, speed symbol, load index, circumference, construction, manufacturer, brand (the manual pairs brand with tread pattern), and degree of wear. Size, speed symbol and load index have to follow the tire inflation pressure label on the driver's door pillar.

If they are not the same, Subaru says serious mechanical damage could be caused to the drivetrain, and that ride, handling, braking, speedometer and odometer calibration, and the clearance between the body and the tires can all be affected. The manual also says the mismatch may be dangerous and can lead to loss of vehicle control. That is one car. Your manual is the one that counts. This page is how to read that kind of warning, not a substitute for it.

Same size code is not the same circumference

Subaru lists circumference and degree of wear as their own lines, after size. A new tire and a worn tire can carry the identical size string and still not be the same height, because the tread that wore away was part of the outside diameter. The manual does not print a universal fraction such as a maximum difference in 32nds. It says the degree of wear should be the same.

The same manual's stability section says all four wheels should be fitted with tires of the same size, type and brand, and that the amount of wear should be the same, for the vehicle dynamics control system and for X Mode. If the all wheel drive warning light flashes, Subaru says to park in a safe place and check whether all four tires are the same diameter and whether any tire has a puncture or has lost air. Continued driving with that light flashing can lead to powertrain damage. A temporary spare can make the light flash. Use the spare the car came with, and only for the trip the manual allows.

We did not find, in the manual we opened, a national number you can apply to every all wheel drive car. If your book prints one, use the book.

What another maker, and USTMA, say when the book is quiet

A Toyota owner's manual chapter for E Four, the electronic on demand all wheel drive system, says to make sure all tires are the specified size, brand, tread pattern and total load capacity, and that they are inflated to the recommended pressure. It says the ABS, TRAC, trailer sway control and vehicle stability control will not function correctly if different tires are installed, and it tells you to contact a Toyota dealer when you replace tires or wheels. The chapter does not name one model in the extract we read, so treat it as Toyota's rule for vehicles that use that system, and confirm the page in your own book.

The USTMA Tire Care and Safety Guide covers the case where the manual says nothing about mixing. Do not mix tire sizes. All four tires must be marked with the same size unless the vehicle manufacturer says otherwise, and that includes a dedicated winter set. Do not mix tread pattern types such as all terrain and all season. The guide then says that for four wheel drive and all wheel drive, even small differences in outside diameter may cause drivetrain damage or mechanical malfunction, and that some manufacturers specifically advise against replacing fewer than all four tires.

USTMA still has a rear axle rule for cars that are not under that stricter advice. Replacing all four is preferred. If you replace two, put the newer pair on the rear. If you replace one, pair it with the deepest remaining tread and put both on the rear. On the Subaru we read, that rear axle compromise does not satisfy "the same degree of wear." Read mixing tires and then the manual, in that order only if the manual is the last word.

Pick the tread only after the four match

NHTSA TireWise says to buy the original size or another size the vehicle manufacturer recommends, read from the door label or the manual. All season tires have some mud and snow capability. Winter tires are more effective than all season tires in deep snow. Summer tires are not for temperatures below freezing or for snow and ice. USTMA says that if winter tires go on the front, they must also go on the rear, and that this includes all wheel drive.

The Crosstrek manual we opened says the car comes with all season tires, and that a winter set must be four tires of the same size, construction, brand and load range. It also says not to mix radial, belted bias and bias construction. One more limit that is easy to over apply: that manual says conventional tire chains cannot be used on that vehicle because there is not enough clearance, and that an SAE class S traction device may be used on the front wheels only if the device maker says it fits. That is a Crosstrek clearance rule, not a rule for every all wheel drive car. Your book may allow chains, or ban them, or require them on a mountain pass. NHTSA's winter driving tips still apply either way: pressure falls when the temperature falls, use the door label rather than the number on the tire, and keep at least 2/32 inch of tread.

For ice specifically, the snowflake and the ice grip symbol are different tests. That comparison is which tire for ice. For one set that still carries the snowflake, see all weather vs all season. Copy the size before you shop, using the placard checklist.

Frequently asked questions

Do all four tires have to match on an all wheel drive car?

On the cars whose manuals we opened, yes. The 2023 Subaru Crosstrek manual requires the same size, speed symbol, load index, circumference, construction, manufacturer, brand and tread pattern, and the same degree of wear. A Toyota E Four chapter requires the specified size, brand, tread pattern and load capacity. USTMA says that if your manual is silent, still do not mix sizes.

Can I replace just one tire?

USTMA says a single new tire can affect suspension, gearing, the transmission and tread wear, and that some manufacturers advise against replacing fewer than four. Subaru lists circumference and degree of wear separately from the size code, so a new tire next to three worn tires fails that list even when the size string matches. Read your own manual before you buy one.

Is there one national limit for how different the tread can be?

Not on the pages opened for this guide. Subaru requires the same circumference and the same degree of wear. USTMA says even a small outside diameter difference may damage a four wheel drive or all wheel drive drivetrain, and tells you to follow the manual. If your manual prints a fraction, use that fraction.

Does the stability system care about mismatched tires?

The Toyota E Four chapter says the ABS, TRAC, trailer sway control and vehicle stability control will not function correctly if different tires are installed. Subaru says a flashing all wheel drive warning can mean the four tires are not the same diameter, and that continued driving can damage the powertrain.

Which tread should the four matching tires be?

Match the set first, then pick the tread for the climate. NHTSA says winter tires are more effective than all season tires in deep snow. USTMA says winter tires on the front axle must also be on the rear, including all wheel drive.
