Which tire should I get for a sports car?

If it stays warm where you drive, a max performance summer tire gives the most grip. If you need one tire year round, an ultra high performance all season tire trades some grip for cold weather use. Below about 45°F, summer tires should come off.

Last updated: November 21, 2026

By Smart Tire Picks Editorial Team

This page is for general information only and is not professional, safety, or legal advice. Always confirm tire size, load index, speed rating, and other specs against your vehicle’s door placard and owner’s manual, and have tires installed by a licensed professional. We have not lab tested products. See our Disclaimer.

The short answer

Tire Rack's category test reported that its max performance summer tires out braked the other categories by 13 feet or more and called them the go to for peak wet performance, while giving up cold weather use and some comfort. Ultra high performance all season tires were a step up from touring tires in grip, but gave up ride quality and were a step down in snow (Tire Rack category test). Michelin says summer tires work best above about 45°F and should come off before sustained cold (Michelin).

The speed letter is a lab category

Checked again 21 November 2026. If the placard or the owner's manual specifies speed rated tires, USTMA says the replacement needs the same or a higher speed rating to keep the vehicle's speed capability. A lower rating means the car's speed has to stay within that tire, and handling can change. The same page says a speed rating does not mean the car can be driven safely at that maximum in bad weather, or if the tire is underinflated, overloaded, worn out, damaged, or altered. On a sports car this is the check that comes before the tread category. Read it next to the load index and speed rating guide.

How to choose

  1. Warm climate, spirited driving: max performance summer.
  2. Mostly dry track days: extreme performance summer, which Tire Rack describes as focused on dry grip with more noise, a stiff ride and a tendency to hydroplane in standing water (Tire Rack).
  3. One tire all year: ultra high performance all season, plus a winter set if you get snow.
  4. Staggered sizes: many sports cars run different front and rear sizes. Keep the maker's sizes; see staggered fitment.

What to avoid

  • Summer tires in the cold. Michelin says their rubber hardens below about 45°F and loses grip on every surface, not just snow (Michelin).
  • Dropping the speed rating. USTMA says replacements need an equal or higher speed rating to keep the vehicle's speed capability (USTMA).
  • Mixing summer and all season tires. Michelin says all four should be the same type (Michelin).

Size, load and speed checks before you buy

  • Size: USTMA says replacement tires should be the original equipment size or an approved option from the vehicle or tire maker, and never smaller or with less load capacity than the original size at placard pressure (USTMA Care and Service).
  • Load index: match or exceed the load capacity on the placard. See load index and speed rating and XL vs SL.
  • Speed rating: USTMA says the replacement speed rating must be equal to or greater than what the vehicle maker specifies to keep the vehicle's speed capability; a lower rated tire limits the vehicle to that tire's speed (USTMA).
  • All four alike: USTMA recommends all four tires share size, load index, speed rating and construction, and says never mix passenger tires with light truck tires on one vehicle (USTMA).

Frequently asked questions

Can I drive summer tires in winter?

Michelin says no: summer tire rubber hardens below about 45°F, reducing grip even on cold dry roads, and grip drops severely in snow or ice.

Are ultra high performance all season tires good in snow?

They are all season tires, but in Tire Rack's category test they were a clear step down in snow and ice from the touring all season tires.

What is the difference between max performance and extreme performance summer tires?

Tire Rack says max performance summer tires lead in the wet and balance some comfort and tread life, while extreme performance summer tires chase dry grip with more noise and a stiffer ride.

Do I need a Y or W speed rating?

You need at least the speed rating the vehicle maker specifies. USTMA says a lower rating limits the vehicle to the tire's speed capability.
