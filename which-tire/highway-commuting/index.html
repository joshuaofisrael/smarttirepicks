Which tire should I get for highway commuting?

For a car that racks up highway miles, a touring all season tire is the usual match. Grand touring leans toward quiet, comfort and handling; standard touring leans toward long tread life.

Last updated: November 16, 2026

By Smart Tire Picks Editorial Team

This page is for general information only and is not professional, safety, or legal advice. Always confirm tire size, load index, speed rating, and other specs against your vehicle’s door placard and owner’s manual, and have tires installed by a licensed professional. We have not lab tested products. See our Disclaimer.

The short answer

Tire Rack calls grand touring all season the go to category for most people: pretty good at everything without being the best at any one thing. Standard touring all season tires focus on tread life, and in Tire Rack's test one standard touring tire took longer to stop from 60 mph than the grand touring tires (Tire Rack category test). Tire Rack describes grand touring all season tires as focused on comfort, road noise reduction and long tread life (Tire Rack).

How to choose

  1. Quiet and comfort first: grand touring all season.
  2. Lowest cost per mile first: standard touring all season, and compare UTQG treadwear. Under federal rules, a tire graded 150 wears one and a half times as well as one graded 100 on the government test course (49 CFR 575.104). Your real miles will differ; see UTQG.
  3. Wet roads: compare the UTQG traction grade (AA, A, B, C). It reflects straight ahead wet braking only, not cornering or hydroplaning (49 CFR 575.104).
  4. Snow on the commute: look at 3PMSF all weather options. See snow without winter tires.

What to avoid

  • A lower speed rating than the placard. USTMA says the vehicle is then limited to the tire's speed capability and handling may change (USTMA).
  • Treating speed ratings as permission. USTMA says a speed rating does not mean the vehicle can be safely driven at that speed, and ratings do not apply to underinflated, overloaded, worn or damaged tires (USTMA).
  • Skipping pressure checks before long trips. USTMA says to check pressure at least monthly and before long trips (USTMA). See tire pressure.

Size, load and speed checks before you buy

  • Size: USTMA says replacement tires should be the original equipment size or an approved option from the vehicle or tire maker, and never smaller or with less load capacity than the original size at placard pressure (USTMA Care and Service).
  • Load index: match or exceed the load capacity on the placard. See load index and speed rating and XL vs SL.
  • Speed rating: USTMA says the replacement speed rating must be equal to or greater than what the vehicle maker specifies to keep the vehicle's speed capability; a lower rated tire limits the vehicle to that tire's speed (USTMA).
  • All four alike: USTMA recommends all four tires share size, load index, speed rating and construction, and says never mix passenger tires with light truck tires on one vehicle (USTMA).

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between grand touring and standard touring tires?

Tire Rack says standard touring all season tires focus on tread life, while grand touring all season tires are the all round choice with an emphasis on comfort and handling. In its test, a standard touring tire needed more distance to stop from 60 mph than the grand touring tires.

Does a higher treadwear number mean more miles?

On the government test course, yes, relatively: a 150 wears one and a half times as well as a 100. Real mileage depends on alignment, inflation, load, roads and driving.

Do I need a higher speed rating for highway driving?

Match or exceed the speed rating the vehicle maker specifies. USTMA says a speed rating is not permission to drive at that speed.

Is a performance tire better for the highway?

Not usually for commuting. Tire Rack found ultra high performance all season tires gave up ride comfort and winter traction compared with touring tires.
