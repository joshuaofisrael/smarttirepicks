Which tire should I get for mountain driving and chain laws?

A tire with the mountain snowflake meets a packed snow test and carries the mud and snow letters. It does not replace chains when the sign requires them. California, Colorado, and Washington write that sign differently, and Colorado's own pages do not all agree.

Last updated: November 21, 2026

By Smart Tire Picks Editorial Team

This page is for general information only and is not professional, safety, or legal advice. Always confirm tire size, load index, speed rating, and other specs against your vehicle's door placard and owner's manual, and have tires installed by a licensed professional. Chain and traction rules change. Follow the sign posted on the road. We have not lab tested products. See our Disclaimer.

The short answer

Buy a tire that can pass the traction rule for the state you will actually drive, and still carry chains or the approved device that state can demand when the sign changes. The mountain snowflake means the tire met a packed snow test. It does not cancel a chain sign.

California, Colorado, and Washington do not use the same words. Colorado's own pages do not even agree with each other. This is a reading of the pages linked below, opened 21 November 2026. The sign on the highway that day outranks this summary.

What the snowflake measures

USTMA's TISB 37 defines passenger and light truck tires for severe snow use. The tire has to reach a snow grip index of at least 1.10 against the 14 inch standard reference tire, or at least 1.12 against the 16 inch reference tire, using a medium packed snow traction test. Tires that qualify are marked with an M+S style legend plus a mountain with a snowflake. The care guide calls an M+S mark by itself a sign of limited mud and snow service, and it points to this bulletin for the snowflake.

That is a snow test. The bulletin we opened does not call the symbol an ice rating, a chain exemption, or a grade under UTQG. 49 CFR 575.104 does not apply to deep tread winter type snow tires, so a winter tire can be legal with no treadwear number. NHTSA's winter page still points shoppers at the uniform grades for comparison. Use the grades when the tire has them. Absence of a number on a deep tread winter tire is the exclusion, not a score of zero. More on the symbol is in winter tires and all weather versus all season.

California posts three chain levels

The Caltrans chain controls page says you stop and put on chains when the signs say chains are required. When chains are required, the speed limit is 25 or 30 miles an hour. Caltrans does not sell or recommend chain brands. It says to check the vehicle maker's specifications. Signs and instructions from Caltrans or the California Highway Patrol control even when they differ from a broadcast.

Level on the Caltrans pageWho must use chains or traction devices
Requirement 1 (shown as R1)Chains on all vehicles except passenger vehicles and light duty trucks under 6,000 pounds gross weight with snow tires on at least two drive wheels. Those vehicles must still carry chains. Vehicles towing trailers need chains on one drive axle. Trailers with brakes need chains on at least one axle.
Requirement 2 (shown as R2)Chains or traction devices on all vehicles except four wheel drive and all wheel drive vehicles with snow tread tires on all four wheels. Those vehicles must still carry traction devices in chain control areas.
Requirement 3 (shown as R3)Chains or traction devices on all vehicles, with no exceptions. Caltrans says R1 and R2 are the common conditions, and a highway will often be closed before R3.

Caltrans quotes the California Vehicle Code definition of a snow tread tire: a relatively deep and aggressive tread compared with a conventional passenger tread. The page says you identify it by MS, M/S, M+S, or the words mud and snow on the sidewall. That definition is the letters plus the tread, not the snowflake by name. A severe snow tire carries an M+S style mark beside the snowflake, so it has the letters. The page we opened does not say the snowflake alone is what the checkpoint looks for.

Colorado: the statute, then three official pages that do not match

The 2025 text of the Colorado highway restriction statute, as posted on Justia and opened 21 November 2026, says that when icy or snow packed conditions exist, the department may restrict a state highway unless the vehicle is equipped with one of these:

  • Tire chains or an alternate traction device.
  • Four wheel drive, with tires of at least three sixteenths of an inch of tread that are imprinted with a mountain snowflake, an M&S, M+S, or M/S symbol, or that are all weather rated by the manufacturer.
  • All wheel drive, with the same tread depth and the same tire marks.

A tire only path for a two wheel drive vehicle is not written in that subsection. The statute defines "equipped" as using or carrying the equipment. It defines an alternate traction device as one the department has approved as comparable to metal chains or tire cables. It says a restriction is effective when signs, including temporary or electronic signs, are up. On Interstate 70 between milepost 133 at Dotsero and milepost 259 at Morrison, that restriction is effective from 1 September through 31 May each year. It is unlawful to proceed when a restriction is in effect without the required equipment. The history note on the page says the tire language was amended in 2025, effective 6 August 2025.

Three public pages, all from Colorado agencies, do not tell that story the same way:

  • CDOT's traction and chain law page says that during a traction law, passenger vehicles need tires with 3/16 inch tread rated all weather, or winter tires with the mountain snowflake or an M+S or M/S mark, or they must carry chains or an approved device. It does not split two wheel drive from all wheel drive in that sentence. It recommends all wheel drive or four wheel drive in the mountains. For the passenger vehicle chain law, it says every vehicle must have chains or an approved device. It lists fines of a $100 fine plus a $33 surcharge, and a $500 fine plus a $157 surcharge if the failure closes a travel lane.
  • CDOT's 20 November 2025 news release says the updated law's tire spec applies to all wheel drive and four wheel drive vehicles, and that a vehicle which does not meet it needs chains or an approved device on two or more drive tires. The boxed summary repeats that split. The same release says the penalty for failing to comply is a $50 fine and a $17 surcharge. That is not the fine printed on the traction law page above.
  • The Colorado State Patrol chain law page says a two wheel drive vehicle has no tire only option, and that chains or an approved device must be physically affixed when the overhead signs are on. It says four wheel drive and all wheel drive can comply with the winter rated tires alone if tread is at least 3/16 inch. It repeats the $100 plus $33 fine, and the $500 plus $157 fine if a lane closes. The page names a 2025 senate bill and a 2026 house bill. We opened the statute and the 2025 change. We did not open the 2026 house bill, so this page does not treat that bill as a source.

What the documents do agree on is narrower. The passenger vehicle chain law, which CDOT describes as the last step before a closure, requires chains or an approved device. The State Patrol page says that applies on two or more drive tires for two wheel drive, four wheel drive, and all wheel drive alike. Tread of at least 3/16 inch appears on the statute, the CDOT pages, and the State Patrol page. Because the fine amounts and the two wheel drive rule are not the same on every official page, treat the posted sign and the statute as the authority, and treat this paragraph as a map of the disagreement.

Washington uses four posted levels, and a lower tread number

The WSDOT winter driving guide spells the advisories out:

  • Traction tires advised: oversize loads are prohibited.
  • Traction tires required: passenger vehicles need approved traction tires. Chains are required on vehicles with a gross vehicle weight rating over 10,000 pounds.
  • Tire chains required: all vehicles need approved chains, except four wheel drive and all wheel drive. Those vehicles must still carry chains or face a violation. The guide cites a $500 fine and the Washington administrative code section it prints for chain rules.
  • Chains required on all vehicles: in extreme weather every vehicle, including four wheel drive and all wheel drive, must install chains.

The guide's note says an approved traction tire has at least an eighth of an inch of tread and is labeled M+S, All Season, or with the mountain snowflake. Installing approved chains makes any tire a traction tire. Studded tires do not meet a chain requirement. Studs are allowed from 1 November through 31 March, the guide says there is no individual exception, and it lists a $136 fine. The guide says anyone on a high elevation road or in winter conditions should carry chains.

WSDOT's 22 October 2026 news release repeats the stud dates, says studs do not count as chains, and says drivers still install chains over studs where chains are posted. It describes stud free winter tread tires as different from all season tires, legal all year, and marked with the mountain snowflake and at least 1/8 inch of tread. It says a manufacturer recommendation against chains does not excuse the state requirement, and that failing to install chains when required risks a $500 fine enforced by the Washington State Patrol. The winter driving guide names approved alternatives for passenger vehicles and points readers to the patrol's equipment page. This site does not rank those products.

Put the three states next to each other and the tire is not one object. Washington will accept an all season label with 1/8 inch of tread when traction tires are required. Colorado's statute, for the tire option that is written for four wheel drive and all wheel drive, asks for 3/16 inch and a snowflake, an M+S style mark, or an all weather rating. California's snow tread definition, as Caltrans quotes it, is a deep aggressive tread plus the mud and snow letters, and Requirement 1 still says those vehicles carry chains.

How to choose the tire, then the hardware

If the trip includes packed snow, a tire with the mountain snowflake meets the severe snow definition and also carries the M+S style letters those states recognize in one form or another. If the only posted Washington level you expect is "traction tires required," the guide's approved traction tire can be an all season label with 1/8 inch of tread. That tire is a weak answer for a Colorado traction restriction, where the written tire option is deeper tread and a winter, mud and snow, or all weather mark, and where two wheel drive may have no tire only path at all.

Chains or an approved device belong in the car before the pass, sized for the tire and allowed by the vehicle maker. Caltrans says to check the maker's specifications. Washington's news release says a maker's advice against chains does not erase the sign. Practice the fit at home. USTMA says if chains are used on dual tires, there has to be enough clearance that the chains do not damage the tires. A compact car rarely has duals. A truck on the same pass might.

USTMA recommends all four tires share size, load index, speed rating, and construction. On four wheel drive and all wheel drive, if the manual is silent, the guide says not to mix sizes, not to mix radial and non radial tires, and not to mix tread types such as all terrain and all season. Even small outside diameter differences can damage the drivetrain. Winter tires go on all four positions when you can. If they are on the front, they must also be on the rear, including on a front wheel drive car. See mixing tires. A Jeep style vehicle that lives on these roads also has a tread choice in which tire for a Jeep style 4x4. The chain sign still applies.

NHTSA's winter driving page, read 21 November 2026, says that in 2024 there were 365 fatal crashes and an estimated 24,383 injury crashes in snow or sleet conditions, and an estimated 131,698 police reported crashes in those conditions. It says inflation pressure falls as the temperature falls, and that the pressure to use is the door label, not the number on the tire. It says tread should be at least 2/32 inch. Those are the checks that sit under whatever symbol the state asks you to show.

Frequently asked questions

Does the mountain snowflake mean I can skip chains?

No. USTMA TISB 37 is a packed snow traction test with a minimum snow grip index of 1.10 or 1.12. California, Colorado, and Washington can still require chains when the sign says so. Read the sign posted that day.

What is California Requirement 1?

Caltrans says chains are required except on passenger vehicles and light duty trucks under 6,000 pounds gross weight with snow tires on at least two drive wheels, and those vehicles must still carry chains. Caltrans quotes a snow tread tire as a deep aggressive tread with MS, M/S, M+S, or the words mud and snow on the sidewall.

Can a two wheel drive car meet Colorado's traction law with tires alone?

The statute text opened 21 November 2026 lists a tire option for four wheel drive and for all wheel drive, with at least 3/16 inch of tread and a mountain snowflake, an M+S style mark, or an all weather rating. It also lists chains or an alternate traction device. A tire only path for two wheel drive is not written there. CDOT's consumer page still describes a tire option for passenger vehicles generally, and the State Patrol page says two wheel drive has no tire only option. Follow the sign.

What does Washington count as an approved traction tire?

The WSDOT winter driving guide says at least 1/8 inch of tread and a label of M+S, All Season, or the mountain snowflake. Studded tires do not meet a chain requirement. Studs are allowed from 1 November through 31 March.

Should winter tires go on all four wheels?

USTMA says winter tires are preferable on all wheel positions. If they are installed on the front, they must also be installed on the rear. That includes front wheel drive cars. The guide also says not to mix all terrain and all season tread on four wheel drive or all wheel drive when the manual is silent.
