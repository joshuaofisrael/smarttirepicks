Which tire for snow if you don't want winter tires?

If you want one set of tires year round and still see snow, choose an all weather tire with the three peak mountain snowflake (3PMSF) symbol. An M+S marking alone does not mean the tire passed a snow test.

Last updated: November 16, 2026

By Smart Tire Picks Editorial Team

This page is for general information only and is not professional, safety, or legal advice. Always confirm tire size, load index, speed rating, and other specs against your vehicle’s door placard and owner’s manual, and have tires installed by a licensed professional. We have not lab tested products. See our Disclaimer.

The short answer

Look for 3PMSF on the sidewall. Michelin says all weather tires carry the 3PMSF symbol, which requires passing a standardized severe snow traction test, while all season tires carry M+S, which is based on tread geometry and not a performance test (Michelin). In Tire Rack's category test, the 3PMSF all weather tire was well ahead of the other all season tires in snow, but a dedicated winter tire was ahead of everything (Tire Rack).

What 3PMSF actually means

USTMA's severe snow definition requires a snow grip index of at least 1.10 or 1.12 (depending on the reference tire) against a standard reference test tire on medium packed snow, using ASTM F1805. Tires that pass may be marked with M+S plus a three peak mountain pictograph with a snowflake inside (USTMA TISB 37). Michelin notes the test does not cover pure ice (Michelin).

When one set is not enough

  • Regular ice or long deep cold. Michelin says you likely need winter tires where it regularly approaches freezing or gets heavy snow or ice (Michelin). Tire Rack's ice braking test favored the dedicated winter tire by a wide margin (Tire Rack).
  • You must drive whatever the weather. A winter set on all four wheels is the stronger choice. See winter tires.

What to avoid

  • Treating M+S as a snow rating. USTMA describes M+S as intended for limited mud and snow service (USTMA).
  • Winter or snow tires on one axle only. USTMA says if winter tires go on the front, they must go on the rear too (USTMA).
  • Worn tread. NHTSA says tread should be at least 2/32 inch on all tires, and suggests considering snow tires (NHTSA winter tips). More tread helps in snow; see tread depth.
  • Summer tires in winter. Michelin says summer tire rubber hardens below about 45°F and loses grip even on dry roads (Michelin).

Size, load and speed checks before you buy

  • Size: USTMA says replacement tires should be the original equipment size or an approved option from the vehicle or tire maker, and never smaller or with less load capacity than the original size at placard pressure (USTMA Care and Service).
  • Load index: match or exceed the load capacity on the placard. See load index and speed rating and XL vs SL.
  • Speed rating: USTMA says the replacement speed rating must be equal to or greater than what the vehicle maker specifies to keep the vehicle's speed capability; a lower rated tire limits the vehicle to that tire's speed (USTMA).
  • All four alike: USTMA recommends all four tires share size, load index, speed rating and construction, and says never mix passenger tires with light truck tires on one vehicle (USTMA).

Frequently asked questions

Are all weather tires as good as winter tires in snow?

No. In Tire Rack's 2024 category test, the 3PMSF all weather tire was clearly better in snow than the other all season tires, but the dedicated winter tire was better than everything, including on ice.

Is M+S good enough for snow?

M+S means the tire is intended for limited mud and snow service. Michelin says it is based on tread design, not a snow traction test. The 3PMSF symbol is the one that requires a packed snow test.

Does 3PMSF mean the tire is good on ice?

Not necessarily. Michelin says the 3PMSF test is on medium packed snow and does not cover pure ice.

Can I keep all weather tires on in summer?

All weather tires are designed for year round use without a seasonal swap, which is the point of the category. Check the speed rating and load index match your placard.
